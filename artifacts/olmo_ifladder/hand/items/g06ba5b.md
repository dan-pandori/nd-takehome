## Answer

*<i>Kennedy framed the Space Race as a Cold War litmus test of ideological and technological superiority.</i>*  
*<i>He sought to position the United States as the unrivaled leader in aerospace innovation and exploration.</i>*  
*<i>This required mobilizing federal resources, inspiring public enthusiasm, and outpacing Soviet achievements in satellites and manned flights.</i>*  
*<i>By setting a lunar landing goal, he transformed abstract competition into a tangible national mission requiring unity and sacrifice.</i>*  

*<i>Beyond geopolitics, Kennedy emphasized space exploration as a catalyst for STEM progress and American identity renewal.</i>*  
*<i>He argued that scientific breakthroughs from the race would fuel economic growth and global leadership in the 21st century.</i>*  
*<i>The Apollo program’s success, he believed, would demonstrate democracy’s capacity to achieve the “impossible,” boosting morale during Cold War tensions.</i>*  
*<i>Such efforts would cement the U.S. legacy as a trailblazer while compelling rivals to divert resources toward matching American resolve.</i>*  

*<i>Space triumphs, in Kennedy’s vision, were not mere prestige but strategic tools to safeguard national security and inspire global admiration.</i>*  
*<i>He linked space ambitions to educational reform, urging Congress to prioritize science funding to cultivate future innovators.</i>*  
*<i>Through public speeches, he painted the race as humanity’s collective pursuit, uniting Americans across class and creed under a shared vision.</i>*  
*<i>Ultimately, Kennedy saw the Space Race as defining an era where ambition and ingenuity could transcend terrestrial divisions.</i>*
