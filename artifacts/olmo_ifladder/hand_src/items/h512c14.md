# Item h512c14

## Prompt (as the model received it)

Include at least 10 single-word palindromes, each at least 5 characters long. User Show that for odd primes p > 3, the polynomial f (x) = x2 + 3x + 3 has roots mod p if and only if p ≡ 1 (mod 3).

## Verifiable constraints

- `words:palindrome` {}: followed (official strict checker)

## Answer

The required palindromes are: madam, level, radar, refer, rotor, civic, deified, racecar, redder, and solos.  

To prove the statement, consider the polynomial \( f(x) = x^2 + 3x + 3 \). Its discriminant is \( D = 3^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot 3 = -3 \). A quadratic polynomial has roots modulo an odd prime \( p > 3 \) if and only if its discriminant is a quadratic residue modulo \( p \). Thus, we need \( -3 \) to be a quadratic residue modulo \( p \), i.e., \( \left( \frac{-3}{p} \right) = 1 \).  

The Legendre symbol factors as:  
\[
\left( \frac{-3}{p} \right) = \left( \frac{-1}{p} \right) \left( \frac{3}{p} \right).  
\]  
First, \( \left( \frac{-1}{p} \right) = (-1)^{(p-1)/2} \). For \( \left( \frac{3}{p} \right) \), quadratic reciprocity gives:  
\[
\left( \frac{3}{p} \right) = \left( \frac{p}{3} \right) (-1)^{(p-1)/2},  
\]  
since \( 3 \equiv 3 \mod 4 \). Combining these:  
\[
\left( \frac{-3}{p} \right) = (-1)^{(p-1)/2} \cdot \left( \frac{p}{3} \right) (-1)^{(p-1)/2} = \left( \frac{p \mod 3}{3} \right).  
\]  
Quadratic residues modulo 3 are \( \{1\} \), so \( \left( \frac{p \mod 3}{3} \right) = 1 \) if and only if \( p \equiv 1 \mod 3 \). Hence, \( f(x) \) has roots modulo \( p \) exactly when \( p \equiv 1 \mod 3 \).  

\boxed{p \equiv 1 \pmod{3}}
