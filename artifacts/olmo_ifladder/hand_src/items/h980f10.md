<em>National Security and Defense</em>  
The Conservative Party of Japan emphasizes robust national security through a strong U.S.-Japan alliance and seeks reinterpretations of constitutional limitations to expand the role of the Self-Defense Forces (SDF). While the party supports the pacifist Constitution’s Article 9, which renounces war, it advocates for a broader interpretation to allow collective self-defense and participate in international peacekeeping missions. This stance reflects concerns over regional threats, particularly from China’s military assertiveness in the East and South China Seas. The party also prioritizes countering North Korea’s nuclear program and maintaining deterrence in light of Japan’s territorial disputes with Russia over the Southern Kuril Islands.  

<em>Economic Policy and Market Liberalization</em>  
The party champions free-market principles, private enterprise, and deregulation to drive economic growth, reflecting its historical ties to Japan’s post-war industrialization. It supports corporate welfare, export-led strategies, and infrastructure development, often prioritizing stability over radical reform. While advocating fiscal conservatism, the party has also pursued large-scale infrastructure projects and monetary easing (e.g., Abenomics under Shinzo Abe), though these policies sometimes clash with its traditional emphasis on balanced budgets. The party opposes extensive social safety nets, preferring market-driven solutions to age-related economic challenges like labor shortages and pension sustainability.  

<em>Social Traditionalism and Cultural Values</em>  
Rooted in Confucian principles and historical continuity, the party emphasizes traditional family structures, patriotism, and societal harmony. It resists rapid social reforms such as gender equality legislation, same-sex marriage, and LGBTQ+ rights, framing them as incompatible with Japanese values. The party supports state-influenced education that highlights national pride, historical narratives (e.g., WWII), and moral education to reinforce communal cohesion. However, it has inconsistently addressed issues like the aging population, often sidestepping calls for immigration as a demographic solution despite economic dependence on foreign labor.  

<em>Constitutional Interpretation and Pacifism</em>  
While upholding the Constitution’s renunciation of war, the party argues for reinterpretations to legitimize a more assertive military posture. This includes debates over “collective self-defense” interpretations since 2014, allowing SDF participation in conflicts involving allies—a shift from strict pacifism. The party opposes formal constitutional amendments, which require public referendum, but has pushed for legal tweaks to expand military capabilities. Internal divisions persist between hardliners advocating for explicit revisions and pragmatic leaders favoring reinterpretations. This approach reflects balancing domestic public skepticism about militarization with external pressures to enhance regional deterrence.  

<em>Political Continuity and governance Strategies</em>  
The party prioritizes political stability through coalition-building, bureaucratic patronage, and incremental policy changes. As Japan’s dominant political force since the 1950s, it relies on a network of affiliated organizations (e.g.,(instru)Conservative pelos lobby groups and regional power bases) to maintain influence. It often governs in alliances with smaller parties, particularly regional or ideologically aligned groups, to secure parliamentary majorities. This strategy emphasizes continuity over radical reform, resisting challenges from opposition parties like the Democratic Party of Japan or the Constitutional Democratic Party of Japan, which it portrays as destabilizing. The party also leverages symbolism, such as revering wartime Prime Minister Hideki Tojo, to appeal to nationalist voters.  

By framing policies around security, tradition, and economic pragmatism, the Conservative Party positions itself as a guardian of Japan’s national interests, navigating between domestic conservative values and evolving global geopolitical dynamics.
