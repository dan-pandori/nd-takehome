## Answer (the text after the model's private reasoning, which is not shown; 460 words)

Prove that the product of two primitive polynomials over a Unique Factorization Domain (UFD) \( R \) is primitive.  
**Proof:** Let \( f(x) = a_nx^n + \cdots + a_0 \) and \( g(x) = b_mx^m + \cdots + b_0 \) be primitive polynomials in \( R[x] \). By definition, the greatest common divisor (gcd) of their coefficients is 1. Suppose, for contradiction, that \( fg \) is not primitive. Then there exists a non-unit \( d \in R \) dividing all coefficients of \( fg \). Write \( fg = d \cdot h \), where \( h \in R[x] \). Since \( R \) is a UFD, factor \( d \) into irreducibles: \( d = \pi_1^{k_1} \cdots \pi_s^{k_s} \). Each \( \pi_i \) divides every coefficient of \( fg \), and because \( f \) and \( g \) are primitive, \( \pi_i \) must divide at least one coefficient of \( f \) and at least one coefficient of \( g \). However, if \( \pi_i \) divides all coefficients of \( fg \), it must divide their pairwise products (from \( f \) and \( g \)), contradicting the primitivity of \( f \) or \( g \), which require \( \pi_i \) to divide some coefficient but not all coefficients (as their gcd is 1). Hence, \( fg \) must be primitive.  

Now, show that if \( f, g \in R[x] \) are coprime, their images in \( F[x] \) (where \( F \) is the field of fractions of \( R \)) are also coprime.  
**Proof:** Assume \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime in \( R[x] \), meaning they share no non-constant common divisor. Suppose they are not coprime in \( F[x] \). Then there exists a non-constant polynomial \( h \in F[x] \) dividing both \( f \) and \( g \) in \( F[x] \). Let \( d = \text{gcd}(f, g) \) in \( R[x] \). Since \( R[x] \) is a UFD, factor \( d \) and \( h \) into irreducibles. But \( h \) lies in \( F[x] \), and its denominators (if written over \( R \)) must divide the leading coefficients of \( f \) and \( g \). Because \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime in \( R[x] \), the leading coefficients of \( f \) and \( g \) are units in \( F \), so no non-unit from \( R \) divides them. Thus, \( h \) must have coefficients in \( R \), implying \( h \in R[x] \), contradicting the coprimality of \( f \) and \( g \) in \( R[x] \). Therefore, \( f \) and \( g \) remain coprime in \( F[x] \).
