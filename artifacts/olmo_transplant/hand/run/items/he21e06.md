# Item he21e06

## Prompt (sent to the model verbatim)

Prove the product of two primitive polynomials in a UFD R[x] is primitive. Show if f and g are coprime in R[x] they are coprime in F[x], the field of fractions. The response must start with a verb.

## Answer (the text after the model's private reasoning, which is not shown; 131 words)

Let f and g be primitive polynomials in a UFD R[x]. Assume their product fg is not primitive, so some nonunit d ∈ R divides all coefficients of fg. Factoring d into irreducibles p₁,…,pₙ, pick any prime p dividing d. In the quotient ring R/pR, reduction modulo p yields nonzero polynomials f̄ and ḡ (since f and g are primitive), yet f̄ḡ = 0, contradicting the integral domain property of R/pR. Thus, fg must be primitive.  

For coprime polynomials f and g in R[x], there exist a, b ∈ R[x] such that af + bg = 1. Localizing at nonzero elements of R gives F[x], where this equation remains valid. Hence, 1 lies in the ideal (f, g) in F[x], implying gcd(f,g) in F[x] divides 1—so they are coprime in F[x].
