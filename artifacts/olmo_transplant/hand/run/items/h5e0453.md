## Answer (the text after the model's private reasoning, which is not shown; 113 words)

Pointwise convergence of polynomials \( B_n(x) \) to \( f(x) \) on \([0,1]\) **does not** guarantee uniform convergence. A classic counterexample is \( f(x) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^n x^k = \frac{x}{1-x} \) for \( x \in (0,1) \). Each partial sum \( B_n(x) = \sum_{k=1}^n x^k \) converges pointwise to \( f(x) \), but convergence fails uniformly on \([0,1]\). Truncation error analysis shows \( |B_n(x) - f(x)| = \frac{x^{n+1}}{1-x} \), which remains large near \( x \to 1^- \) for any fixed \( n \), demonstrating non-uniform convergence. Uniform convergence requires equicontinuity (Arzelà-Ascoli) or dominated convergence, absent here. Thus, pointwise behavior alone is insufficient; additional conditions like equicontinuity are necessary for uniformity.
