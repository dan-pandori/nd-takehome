- To show that \( f \) is Riemann integrable on \([0, 5]\):
  - A function is Riemann integrable on \([a, b]\) if it is bounded and continuous almost everywhere (i.e., the set of discontinuities has measure zero).
  - \( f \) is piecewise constant and bounded. The points of discontinuity are at \( x = 1, 2, \) and \( 3 \), where left and right limits differ.
  - These discontinuities are isolated and occur at a finite (and hence measure-zero) set of points, meaning \( f \) is continuous everywhere else on \([0, 5]\).
  - Therefore, \( f \) satisfies the criteria for Riemann integrability.

- To compute the integral \( \int_{0}^{5} f(x) \, dx \):
  - Calculate the area of each constant region of \( f \) and sum them:
    - From \( 0 \) to \( 1 \): \( f(x) = 1 \). Area = \( 1 \times (1 - 0) = 1 \).
    - From \( 1 \) to \( 2 \): \( f(x) = 3 \). Area = \( 3 \times (2 - 1) = 3 \).
    - From \( 2 \) to \( 3 \): \( f(x) = 2 \). Area = \( 2 \times (3 - 2) = 2 \).
    - From \( 3 \) to \( 5 \): \( f(x) = 4 \). Area = \( 4 \times (5 - 3) = 8 \).
  - Summing these areas: \( 1 + 3 + 2 + 8 = 14 \).

- Conclusion:
  - The function \( f \) is Riemann integrable due to its piecewise continuity and finite discontinuities.
  - The integral of \( f \) over \([0, 5]\) is computed as the sum of the areas of the constant segments.
