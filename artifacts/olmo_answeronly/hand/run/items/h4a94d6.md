## Answer (the text after the model's private reasoning, which is not shown; 162 words)

Prove that the product of two primitive polynomials in a UFD \( R[x] \) is primitive by contradiction: suppose \( f(x) \) and \( g(x) \) are primitive, and let \( h(x) = f(x)g(x) \). If a prime \( p \in R \) divides all coefficients of \( h \), then \( h \equiv 0 \) in \( (R/pR)[x] \). Since \( R/pR \) is an integral domain, \( f \) or \( g \) must be zero in \( (R/pR)[x] \), contradicting their primitivity (which requires their coefficients' GCD to be 1, precluding divisibility by \( p \)). Show \( f \) and \( g \) coprime in \( R[x] \) imply coprimality in \( F[x] \): by hypothesis, \( \exists a(x), b(x) \in R[x] \) such that \( a(x)f + b(x)g = 1 \). This identity holds in \( F[x] \), so any common divisor of \( f, g \) in \( F[x] \) divides 1, hence is a unit.
