* The function \( f \) is Riemann integrable on \([0, 5]\) because it is bounded and its discontinuities form a set of measure zero.  
  - \( f \) has values 1, 3, 2, and 4 over its domain, so it is bounded between 1 and 4. Its discontinuities occur only at \( x = 1, 2, \) and \( 3 \), which are isolated points. Since a finite set has Lebesgue measure zero, \( f \) satisfies the Riemann integrability criteria.  

* The integral of \( f \) from 0 to 5 is computed by summing the areas of rectangular regions over each constant subinterval.  
  - The contribution from \([0, 1]\) is \( 1 \times 1 = 1 \), from \((1, 2]\) is \( 3 \times 1 = 3 \), from \((2, 3)\) is \( 2 \times 1 = 2 \), and from \([3, 5]\) is \( 4 \times 2 = 8 \). Adding these gives \( 1 + 3 + 2 + 8 = 14 \).
