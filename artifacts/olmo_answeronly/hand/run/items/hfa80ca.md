INT. EAGLE LUNAR MODULE - DAY  

  Neil Armstrong’s fingers hover over a cyanide capsule. His breath fogs the visor, each exhale a ghost in the void.  

   Buzz Aldrin’s voice shatters the silence: “Neil… the engine won’t fire. We’re marooned.”  

    Sparks erupt from a sparking panel. The cabin’s artificial gravity flickers, sending tools clattering.  

     A crackling transmission pierces the chaos: *“This is Earth… Neil, Buzz…”* Nixon’s voice, strained yet solemn.  

      “…You’ve *reached*, even if this is how you stay.” Static swallows his words.  

       Armstrong’s thumb twists the cyanide vial. “For humanity,” he rasps.  

        Buzz’s hand trembles as he cracks open his own capsule. “No regrets,” he croaks.  

         Nixon’s voice returns, now a requiem: *“Your light… will guide the stars…”*  

          The pills dissolve in their mouths. Sweetness quickly twists to metallic burn.  

           Their eyelids sag, fighting the creeping fog. The module’s lights dim, as if mourning.  

            “Think of the kids…” Buzz slurs, fingers brushing Armstrong’s glove.  

             Armstrong manages a smile—or is it the toxins?—as consciousness unravels.  

              Nixon’s final plea wavers: *“You… are not failed heroes…”*  

               Darkness swallows the men. The module sleeps. Earth turns.  

                Only the moon remains—silent witness to the ultimate sacrifice.
