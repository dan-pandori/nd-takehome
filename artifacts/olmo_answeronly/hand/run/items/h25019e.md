  - The function \( f \) attains constant values (1, 2, 3, or 4) on intervals of length ≥ 0, ensuring boundedness. Its discontinuities occur only at the points \( x = 1, 2, 3 \), which are isolated and thus have Lebesgue measure zero, satisfying the Riemann integrability criteria.  

* The integral of \( f \) from 0 to 5 is 14.

    -- On [0, 1], \( f(x) = 1 \), so the area is \( 1 \times (1 - 0) = 1 \).  
    -- On (1, 2], \( f(x) = 3 \), so the area is \( 3 \times (2 - 1) = 3 \).  
    -- On (2, 3), \( f(x) = 2 \), so the area is \( 2 \times (3 - 2) = 2 \).  
    -- On [3, 5], \( f(x) = 4 \), so the area is \( 4 \times (5 - 3) = 8 \).  
    -- Summing these gives \( 1 + 3 + 2 + 8 = 14 \).
