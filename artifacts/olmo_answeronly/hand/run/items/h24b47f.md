Prove that the product of two primitive polynomials in a UFD \( R[x] \) is primitive by contradiction: let \( f \) and \( g \) be primitive polynomials with product \( h = fg \). Suppose a prime \( p \in R \) divides all coefficients of \( h \). Then in \( R/pR[x] \), the product \( \overline{f} \cdot \overline{g} = \overline{h} = 0 \). Since \( R/pR \) is an integral domain, either \( \overline{f} = 0 \) or \( \overline{g} = 0 \), implying \( p \) divides all coefficients of \( f \) or \( g \), contradicting their primitivity. Thus, \( h \) must be primitive.

Show that coprime polynomials \( f, g \in R[x] \) remain coprime in \( F[x] \): since \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime in \( R[x] \), there exist \( a, b \in R[x] \) such that \( af + bg = 1 \). This identity holds in \( F[x] \), implying any common divisor of \( f \) and \( g \) in \( F[x] \) divides 1, hence must be a unit in \( F[x] \). Therefore, \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime in \( F[x] \).
