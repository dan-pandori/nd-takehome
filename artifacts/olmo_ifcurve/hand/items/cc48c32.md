## Answer

The night yawned its cavernous belly, shielding the flicker of candlelight that clung to the hearth like a moth’s last gasp—*and* oh, how the shadows conspired! For there he sat, a spectral silhouette in the fracas of his own dusk, fingers tracing cracks in the armchair’s spine as if decoding the universe’s runes. *Yet* the question gnawed: could I flee this labyrinth of obligation, cast off the yoke of kinship like a snake shedding its skin, when his sightless eyes, twin voids plugged with the ache of years, still held the echo of my mother’s laugh? *Or* must I remain, steward of acesthel’s embered existence, nursing the embers of his pride, pockets jingling with coins but hearts thin as parchment? The road beckoned with siren whispers—*freedom, fortune, the tang of untried skies*—*but* ‘twas a path paved in the currency of guilt, every step a betrayal of the man who’d cradled me in his calloused palms when the world was a kaleidoscope of shouts and wet aprons. *Nor* could I unsee the way his hands trembled, grasping at the void where supper once stood, the way his voice, now a rasp of wind through reeds, still conjured visions of robust mornings when bread was buttered thick, and the sun gilded his laughter. *For* to abandon him was to let the final thread of warmth fray into nothingness—a cruel joke the fates might ink in braille for his trembling fingers to trace. *And* yet… *ah, but* what of my own marrow? Did not the blood that thrummed in my veins demand more than this slow martyrdom, this silent vigil over a man who might never know whether I stayed out of love or pity? The room blurred—a museum of moments frozen in amber: his hands steadier, his smile wider, the clatter of malts and stories at the tavern, now distant as the hum of a half-remembered psalm. *So* the pendulum swung between *stay* and *flight*, each extreme a blade poised over the wound in my chest—a wound neither choice could staunch, but only deepen. The hearth’s glow dimmed further, as though the universe itself held its breath… waiting.
