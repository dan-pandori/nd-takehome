# Item c9394ae

## Prompt (as the model received it)

particle of mass m moves only along a frictionless, vertical, circular track of radius a. (a) Write the classical energy of this mass as a function of angular momentum, neglecting gravity, and assume that the quantum mechanical Hamiltonian comes from substituting the quantum angular momentum operator. What is the Hamiltonian operator? (b) Write Schrodinger equation in the position representation. The angle φ should figure prominently. (c) Find the energy eigenvalues and energy eigenfunctions in the position representation. Use at least 5 different coordinating conjunctions in the response.

## Verifiable constraints

- `count:conjunctions` {"small_n": 5.0}: followed (official strict checker)

## Answer

The classical energy is given by \( E = \frac{L^2}{2ma^2} \), and the quantum Hamiltonian replaces \( L \) with \( -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial\phi} \), yielding \( H = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial\phi^2} \). But the Schrödinger equation in position space must then include the angular coordinate \( \phi \), so it becomes \( i\hbar \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t} = H\Psi \). Or, when solving for eigenstates, the energy eigenvalues are found to be proportional to \( n^2 \), nor can the eigenfunctions be other than complex exponentials. Thus, these results highlight the role of quantization in angular systems.
