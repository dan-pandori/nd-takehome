### When Did Humans Realize Space Is a Dark Void with Stars and Galaxies?  
The transition from viewing space as a luminous "celestial sphere" to understanding it as a dark void filled with distant stars and galaxies unfolded over centuries:  

#### **Ancient and Medieval Beliefs**  
- **Pre-Scientific Eras**: Many ancient cultures (e.g., Babylonians, Greeks) conceptualized the sky as a **firmament** ordivine dome, often viewed as a luminous blue extension of Earth’s atmosphere. The absence of the sun’s light at night was sometimes attributed to Earth’s shadow or a "darkening" of the celestial sphere.  
- **Aristotle (4th century BCE)**: Proposed a finite, spherical universe with concentric layers between Earth and the "prime mover" (God), but this model still imagined the heavens as spiritually or physically luminous.  

#### **Early Modern Shifts (16th–17th Centuries)**  
- **Galileo Galilei (1609+)**: His telescopic observations revealed the Milky Way as countless stars and the Moon’s cratered surface, undermining the notion of a flawlessly bright celestial sphere. This hinted that **space between stars could be empty/dark**.  
- **Isaac Newton (17th century)**: In *Opticks* (1704), he explained the blue daytime sky via Rayleigh scattering but emphasized that **light from distant stars must traverse vast emptiness** to reach Earth, implying a dark universe punctuated by light sources.  
- **Evolution of Vacuum Theory**: The concept of a **cosmic "vacuum"** (empty space) gained traction in the 17th century (e.g., via Robert Boyle’s work on air pumps), challenging earlier ideas that space was filled with luminous "aether."  

#### **19th–20th Century Confirmations**  
- **Olbers’ Paradox (1823)**: Highlighted the mystery of why the night sky is dark if the universe is infinite and eternal. This spurred debates about the universe’s **finite age** and **expansion**, later resolved by the Big Bang theory (1920s–1930s).  
- **Hubble’s Discoveries (1920s–1930s)**: Observations of **galaxies beyond the Milky Way** (e.g., Andromeda) revealed the universe’s immense scale, confirming that most space is dark, with stars and galaxies as isolated points of light.  

#### **Key Takeaway**  
By the **17th century**, emerging telescope evidence and vacuum theory laid the groundwork for understanding space as a dark void. This matured in the **20th century** with relativity and cosmology, replacing older notions of a uniformly luminous celestial "blue" realm. The shift involved contributions from observation (*Galileo*), physics (*Newton*), cosmology (*Hubble*), and philosophical debates about vacuums and light.
