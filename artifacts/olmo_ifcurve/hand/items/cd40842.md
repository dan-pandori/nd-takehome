## Answer

To explain the relationships between the row space, nullspace, column space, and left nullspace in linear algebra, let’s break down each statement using definitions, examples, and logical deductions. **We** will rely on the standard inner product (dot product) in **ℝⁿ** or **ℝᵐ** for orthogonality.

---

### **1. The Row Space of a Matrix is Orthogonal to Its Nullspace**  
**Consider** an **m×n matrix A**. The **row space** of **A** is the subspace spanned by **A’s rows**, while the **nullspace** (kernel) of **A** is the set of vectors **x ∈ ℝⁿ** such that **Ax = 0**.  

Let’s dissect **Ax = 0**. When you multiply **A** (with its rows as vectors) by **x**, each component of the resulting zero vector is the **dot product** of **x** with a row of **A**:  
- For row **i** of **A**, **x · row_i = 0** (because the *i*th entry of **Ax** is zero).  
- **Therefore**, **x** is orthogonal to **every row of A**.  

Since the **row space** is the span of **A’s rows**, any linear combination of these rows (i.e., any vector in the row space) will also be orthogonal to **x**. **This** is because the dot product is linear:  
> *If row₁, row₂, ..., rowₙ are orthogonal to x, then for any scalars c₁, c₂, ..., cₙ:*  
> **x · (c₁row₁ + c₂row₂ + ... + cₙrowₙ) = c₁(x · row₁) + ... + cₙ(x · rowₙ) = 0.**  

Thus, **x** is orthogonal to **the entire row space**. **The nullspace** consists of **all vectors** like **x** that satisfy this condition, so **the nullspace is contained within the orthogonal complement of the row space**.  

**Dimensionally**, by the Rank-Nullity Theorem:  
- **dim(row space of A) + dim(nullspace) = n** (the number of columns).  
Since the orthogonal complement of the row space has dimension **n - rank(A)** (which equals **nullity**), **the nullspace equals the orthogonal complement of the row space**.  

---

### **2. The Column Space is Orthogonal to the Left Nullspace (of A)**  
**Now**, **the column space** of **A** is the span of **A’s columns**, while **the left nullspace** (nullspace of **Aᵀ**) consists of vectors **y ∈ ℝᵐ** satisfying **yᵀA = 0**.  

**Recall** that transposing **A** swaps rows and columns:  
- **The column space of A = the row space of Aᵀ**.  
- **The left nullspace of A = the nullspace of Aᵀ**.  

**Apply the earlier logic to Aᵀ**:  
- **For Aᵀ**, its **row space** (which is the column space of A) and **nullspace** (the left nullspace of A) must be orthogonal (as established in Part 1).  
- Specifically, any **y** in the nullspace of **Aᵀ** (i.e., **yᵀAᵀ = 0**) satisfies **yᵀ times each row of Aᵀ = 0**. But the rows of **Aᵀ** are **A’s columns**. Thus, **y** is orthogonal to **every column of A**.  

Since the **column space** of **A** is the span of its columns, **y** must be orthogonal to the **entire column space**. Reversing the reasoning: vectors in the column space are orthogonal to **all y** in the left nullspace.  

**Dimensionally**, Rank-Nullity applied to **Aᵀ** gives:  
- **dim(column space of A) + dim(left nullspace of A) = m** (the number of rows of A).  
Thus, **the column space and left nullspace are orthogonal complements in ℝᵐ**.  

---

### **Key Takeaways**  
1. **Orthogonality arises naturally** from the definitions of matrices and their subspaces. The dot product condition **Ax = 0** or **yᵀA = 0** forces vectors to lie in orthogonal subspaces.  
2. **Transpose duality**: Swapping rows ↔ columns (via **Aᵀ**) allows us to use the same reasoning **for column spaces and left nullspaces** as **for row spaces and nullspaces**.  
3. **Geometric intuition**: In **ℝⁿ**, the nullspace "lives" outside the row space; in **ℝᵐ**, the left nullspace "lives" outside the column space. Their orthogonality partitions the space into complementary subspaces.  

**You** might ask: *"Why does this matter?"*  
- **Orthogonality** simplifies computations (e.g., projections onto subspaces).  
- These relationships underpin the **fundamental theorem of linear algebra**, which links the four subspaces (row/column spaces and their nullspaces).  
- **They** also explain why matrices with orthonormal columns (like QR decomposition) have simplified nullspaces.  

By seeing these connections, **you** can better navigate concepts like least-squares solutions (connected to orthogonality between column spaces and residuals) or the singular value decomposition (SVD), which merges these ideas in a powerful framework.
