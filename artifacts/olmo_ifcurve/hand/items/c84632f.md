## Prompt (as the model received it)

Suppose a sequence of polynomials B_n(x) converge pointwise to f(x) on [0, 1]. Does it follow that the B_n converge uniformly? Ensure that stop words constitute no more than 20% of the total words in your response.

## Answer

No. Pointwise convergence of polynomials B_n(x) to f(x) on [0, 1] does not guarantee uniform convergence. Consider B_n(x) = (1 - x)^n. Pointwise, B_n(x) → 0 for x ∈ [0,1), but at x = 1, it remains 0 for all n. Define f(x) as the zero function on [0,1], but even this case shows non-uniformity because |B_n(1/2)| = (1/2)^n → 0, yet near x=0, B_n(x) ≡1. The maximum difference |B_n(x) - f(x)| = 1 at x=0 persists, preventing uniform convergence. Discontinuous limits (e.g., f(x)=0 on [0,1], B_n(1)=1 via B_n(x)=x^n) also demonstrate this. Thus, uniform convergence fails despite pointwise limits.
