## Prompt (as the model received it)

particle of mass m moves only along a frictionless, vertical, circular track of radius a. (a) Write the classical energy of this mass as a function of angular momentum, neglecting gravity, and assume that the quantum mechanical Hamiltonian comes from substituting the quantum angular momentum operator. What is the Hamiltonian operator? (b) Write Schrodinger equation in the position representation. The angle φ should figure prominently. (c) Find the energy eigenvalues and energy eigenfunctions in the position representation. Use at least 5 different coordinating conjunctions in the response.

## Answer

**(a) Classical Energy and Quantum Hamiltonian**  
In classical mechanics, neglecting gravity, the particle's total energy is purely kinetic. The angular momentum \( L \) is given by \( L = ma v \), where \( v \) is the tangential speed. Solving for \( v \) in terms of \( L \) yields \( v = \frac{L}{ma} \). Substituting this into the kinetic energy formula \( E = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \), we find the classical energy:  
\[
E_{\text{classical}} = \frac{L^2}{2ma^2}.
\]  
For the quantum Hamiltonian, the angular momentum operator in the position representation (angle \( \phi \)) is \( \hat{L}_z = -i\hbar \frac{d}{d\phi} \). Substituting \( L \) with this operator in the classical expression gives:  
\[
\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{L}_z^2}{2ma^2}.
\]  
Calculating \( \hat{L}_z^2 \):  
\[
\hat{L}_z^2 = \left(-i\hbar \frac{d}{d\phi}\right)^2 = -\hbar^2 \frac{d^2}{d\phi^2}.
\]  
Thus, the quantum Hamiltonian operator becomes:  
\[
\boxed{\hat{H} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2} \frac{d^2}{d\phi^2}}.
\]  

---

**(b) Schrödinger Equation in Position Representation**  
In position representation (angle \( \phi \)), the time-independent Schrödinger equation reads \( \hat{H}\psi = E\psi \). Substituting the Hamiltonian from (a):  
\[
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2} \frac{d^2\psi(\phi)}{d\phi^2} = E\psi(\phi).
\]  
Rearranging terms:  
\[
\frac{d^2\psi(\phi)}{d\phi^2} + \left(\frac{2ma^2E}{\hbar^2}\right)\psi(\phi) = 0.
\]  
This second-order differential equation governs the angular dependence of \( \psi(\phi) \), where the angle \( \phi \) explicitly shapes the system’s dynamics.  

---

**(c) Energy Eigenvalues and Eigenfunctions**  
To solve the differential equation, we apply periodic boundary conditions \( \psi(\phi + 2\pi) = \psi(\phi) \). Solutions must be single-valued eigenfunctions of \( \hat{L}_z \), leading to eigenvalues quantized by the integer angular momentum quantum number \( l \). Assuming \( \psi_l(\phi) = e^{il\phi} \), substituting into the Schrödinger equation yields:  
\[
-l^2 e^{il\phi} + \frac{2ma^2E}{\hbar^2}e^{il\phi} = 0.
\]  
Solving for \( E \):  
\[
E_l = \frac{\hbar^2l^2}{2ma^2}, \quad l = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \ldots.
\]  
The corresponding normalized eigenfunctions are:  
\[
\boxed{\psi_l(\phi) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{il\phi}}.
\]  

**Key Observations**:  
For each \( l \), the eigenstates represent quantized angular momentum states. Although the system lacks external forces, the constraint of a circular path enforces discrete \( l \) values, leading to quantized energies depending solely on \( l^2 \). Even **without potential energy**, the boundary conditions impose quantization, yielding a spectrum analogous to atomic orbitals. The energies increase quadratically with \( |l| \), and eigenfunctions form an orthogonal basis for expanding arbitrary states on the ring. These results **highlight how topology** (the compact nature of \( \phi \)) **integrates with quantum mechanics** to produce such quantization, demonstrating that **rotational confinement alone** can induce discrete energy levels. **Consequently**, the particle’s angular motion cannot adopt arbitrary momenta but must adhere to these quantized states. This ties classical continuous motion to quantum discreteness via operator substitution and boundary conditions.
