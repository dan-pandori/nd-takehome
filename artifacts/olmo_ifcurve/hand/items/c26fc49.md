### Proof of Riemann Integrability of a Step Function  

Let \( f: [a, b] \to \mathbb{R} \) be a **step function**. By definition, there exist points \( a = x_0 < x_1 < \dots < x_n = b \) and constants \( c_0, c_1, \dots, c_{n-1} \) such that:  
\[
f(x) = 
\begin{cases}
c_0, & \text{if } x \in [x_0, x_1), \\
c_1, & \text{if } x \in [x_1, x_2), \\
& \vdots \\
c_{n-1}, & \text{if } x \in [x_{n-1}, x_n].
\end{cases}
\]

Let \( P = \{x_0, x_1, \dots, x_n\} \) be the partition of \([a, b]\) induced by the step points. For each subinterval \([x_i, x_{i+1}]\), \( f \) is **constant** (equal to \( c_i \)). Therefore:  
- **Upper sum**: \( U(P, f) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} c_i (x_{i+1} - x_i) \).  
- **Lower sum**: \( L(P, f) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} c_i (x_{i+1} - x_i) \).  

Since \( U(P, f) = L(P, f) \), their difference is **zero**:  
\[
U(P, f) - L(P, f) = 0 < \epsilon \quad \text{for any } \epsilon > 0.
\]

This satisfies the Riemann integrability criterion: for any \( \epsilon > 0 \), a partition \( P \) exists (specifically the one aligned with the step discontinuities) making \( U - L < \epsilon \). Hence, **step functions are Riemann integrable**.  

\(\blacksquare\)
