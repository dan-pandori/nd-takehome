## Answer

The afternoon sun slants through lace curtains, casting gold dust over the cluttered living room. A half-eaten pizza cools on the coffee table, and Lily perches on the arm of the couch, swinging her neon pink plastic wand like a tiny conqueror.  
   “Rai, *please*? It’s just for practice! Plus, I’ve been studying *real* magic—and if I don’t get this right, Aunt Mabel’s gonna turn me into a toad again.” She pokes at the wand, which glows faintly like a dying firefly.  
      “Aunt Mabel’s curses are *not* reliable babysitting references!” You grimace, arms crossed. “And turning me into a kid? That’s messed up. What’s next—diapers and naptime?”  
        The wand SHudders violently. A hot, fizzy sensation pricks your skin. Your shoes suddenly feel too big.  
          “Hey, watch—” Lily’s voice shrinks. “Uh-oh. Maybe… maybe try *not* squirming!” The pink light swallows your legs.  
            Your knees buckle. Your jeans pool around your ankles, and the room suddenly smells like crayons and cartoon characters. Your voice, when you try to shout, comes out high-pitched and giggly: “Stop it! I’m too cool to be six!”  
              Lily cackles, twirling the wand like a baton. “Too bad, genius! Now you’re my lil’ bro, and *I’m* in charge!” She tosses a box of Count Chocula cereal at you. It hits your chest, spraying marshmallow bits like confetti.  
                You glance down. Your hands are smaller, fingers stubby, dotted with constellations of freckles you swore weren’t there before. The TV remote is now the size of a lunchbox across the room. A stuffed unicorn looms in the corner, its plastic horn glinting.  
                  “Rai? Who’s Rai?” Lily tilts her head, all fake innocence. “I thought your name was… *Biscuit*?” She outright cackles this time.  
                    The spell’s warmth creeps up your neck. Your thoughts feel… squishier. Less algebra, more *what’s that fluffy thing?!* A sudden craving for cookies claws at your gut. You catch yourself reaching for a teddy bear on the floor.  
                      Lily zeroes in, eyes wide. “Oh *no*, Biscuit. Did somebody lose their big-boy pride?” She flops onto the couch, crossing her legs primly. “Now sit. Mama’s gonna teach you how to brush your *tiny* teeth.” She wiggles a toothbrush menaced by glitter.  
                        Your chest tightens—part defiance, part … fear? The room’s edges blur. Your 17-year-old brain screams *FIGHT THIS*, but the voice is muffled, like it’s underwater. A chip from the cereal crust sticks to your cheek. You hate it. You also kinda want more.  
                          The spell hums softer now. Lily smirks, already flipping through a dog-eared babysitting manual. “Good boy,” she coos. “Now… let’s practice *diaper changes*.” Her grin couldn’t be wider if she’d just won the presidency of chaos.
