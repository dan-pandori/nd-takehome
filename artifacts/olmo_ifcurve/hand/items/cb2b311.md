## Prompt (as the model received it)

Discuss how likely the predictions below are.  • Privacy will become a thing of the past. • By the early 2030s, we will be able to copy human consciousness to a computer. • Wireless Internet connection will be available anywhere on our planet. • All forms of transport will eventually become electric. • The effects of climate change will be irreversible. • Physical money (coins and notes) won't exist anymore. • Scientists will unlock the genetic secrets that could allow us to live for centuries, maybe forever.

## Answer

Privacy is under siege from omnipresent digital tracking, data breaches, and AI-driven surveillance, yet global awareness and regulatory frameworks like GDPR are reinforcing its value—privacy might erode significantly but will remain a fractured fragment of its former self, privacy.  

Consciousness transfer to machines demands decoding brain functions far beyond current neuroscience and computing capabilities; even by the 2030s, creating a faithful replica of subjective awareness seems implausible, leaving consciousness as a frontier of speculation rather than achievement, consciousness.  

Wireless internet’s expansion faces technical limits—spectrum constraints and energy demands in remote regions—but satellite constellations and 6G could realize near-ubiquitous coverage, making wireless connectivity a near certainty in developed areas and incomplete elsewhere, wireless.  

Transport electrification accelerates in passenger cars and short-haul flights, but heavy industries like long-haul aviation and cargo shipping struggle with energy density issues, suggesting transport will remain partially fossil-fueled for decades, transport.  

Effects of climate change, such as coastal flooding and biodiversity loss, are already irreversible, but aggressive decarbonization could avert catastrophic warming scenarios—effects will define the 21st century’s ecological legacy, effects.  

Physical money’s decline is inevitable in tech-savvy regions due to digital payments, yet cash persists in informal economies and as a hedge against cyber vulnerabilities, leaving physical currency a relic of niche use rather than universal disposal, physical.  

Scientists are advancing anti-aging therapies and gene editing to combat disease, but immortality or centuries-long lifespans defy biological plausibility and ethical consensus, rendering scientists’ ability to deliver eternal life highly speculative, scientists.
