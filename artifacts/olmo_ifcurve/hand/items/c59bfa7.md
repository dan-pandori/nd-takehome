### **1. Problem Statement: The Space Debris Crisis**  
- **Context:** Over 34,000 trackable debris objects in Low Earth Orbit (LEO). Collision risk growing exponentially!  
- **Impact:** Threatens operational satellites, future missions, and access to space.

### **2. Research & Existing Solutions**  
- **Active Debris Removal (ADR):** Technologies include nets ("ESA's ClearSpace-1"), robotic arms ("NASA’s Restore-L"), and lasers.  
- **Passive Mitigation:** Drag augmentation using aerosols or tethers.  
- **Gaps:** Most systems target small debris (<10 cm); scalable solutions for larger objects (>10 cm) are lacking.  

---

### **3. Space Law Case Study: The Cosmos 954 Incident (1978)**  
**Span of Dates:** Launched in 1973; re-entry and fragmentation in 1978.  
**Countries/Companies:** Soviet Union (space object owner) vs. Canada (affected nation; U.S. provided intelligence).  
**Major Influences:**  
- **The Outer Space Treaty (1967):** Prohibits weapons in space but no debris liability.  
- **Liability Convention (1972):** Holds states accountable for damage caused by space objects.  
- **Diplomatic Pressure:** USSR agreed to compensate Canada for radioactive debris cleanup (~$4 million CAD).  

**Outcomes if No Space Law:**  
No obligation for compensation; potential conflict over cleanup costs and blame. Environmental damage from scattered radionuclides could have worsened without accountability (?! Imagine radioactive debris unaddressed—Chaos!).

### **4. Design Process: ADR-SAT System**  
#### **Concept: A Net-Based Capture & Deorbiting Satellite**  
**Key Components:**  
- ** deployable Mesh Net (30m x 30m):** Made of Kevlar-reinforced polymer for durability in extreme conditions.  
- **AI-Powered Sensors:** Lidar and radar for object detection and trajectory prediction.  
- **Thruster Array:** 12 ion thrusters for maneuverability and deorbit burns (ΔV ≈ 2 km/s).  
- **Control Module:** Uses open-source software for international collaboration.  

#### **Steps in the Design Process:**  
1. **Problem Definition:** Prioritize debris ≥10 cm due to high collision risk.  
2. **Research:** Evaluated costs, technical feasibility, and precedents (e.g., ClearSpace-1 feasibility studies).  
3. **Concept Development:** Chose nets over robotic arms to reduce complexity/cost.  
4. **Requirements:**  
   - **Efficacy:** Capture 95% of target debris.  
   - **Safety:** Avoid fragmenting debris upon contact.  
   - **Cost:** <$50M per unit (commercially viable with subsidies).  
5. **Mock-Up Design:** A 1:100 scale prototype with foldable net and modular thrusters.  

#### **Why This Design?**  
- **Nets Over Robotics:** Lower technical risk; no need for precision grappling. Proven in lab tests (e.g., NASA’s "Debris snagging" trials).  
- **Sustainability:** Satellite self-destructs post-mission via controlled re-entry; complies with UN space sustainability guidelines.  
- **Collaboration-Ready:** Open-source tech allows global participation (e.g., ESA, NASA, and emerging space nations).
