# Item ca0a814

## Prompt (as the model received it)

Prove the product of two primitive polynomials in a UFD R[x] is primitive. Show if f and g are coprime in R[x] they are coprime in F[x], the field of fractions. The response must start with a verb.

## Verifiable constraints

- `words:start_verb` {}: followed (official strict checker)

## Answer

Prove the product of two primitive polynomials in a UFD \( R[x] \) is primitive by first noting that a primitive polynomial has coefficients with gcd 1. Let \( f, g \in R[x] \) be primitive, so their contents \( \text{Cont}(f) = \text{Cont}(g) = 1 \). The product \( h = fg \) must also have content 1. To see this, suppose a non-unit \( d \in R \) divides all coefficients of \( h \). Factor \( d \) into primes in \( R \), and let \( p \) be one such prime. Since \( p \) divides all coefficients of \( h \), \( p \) must divide all coefficients of \( f \) or \( g \) (as \( (p) \) is prime in \( R[x] \)), contradicting primitiveness of \( f \) or \( g \). Thus, no such \( d \) exists, so \( h \) is primitive.

Next, if \( f, g \in R[x] \) are coprime, then \( \gcd(f, g) = 1 \) in \( R[x] \). In \( F[x] \) (where \( F \) is the field of fractions of \( R \)), any common divisor \( d \in F[x] \) of \( f \) and \( g \) can be written \( d = p/q \) with \( p, q \in R[x] \), \( \gcd(p, q) = 1 \). Since \( d \) divides \( f \) in \( F[x] \), we have \( f = (p/q)s \) for some \( s \in F[x] \). Clearing denominators, \( f q = p s \) in \( R[x] \), implying \( p \) divides \( f \). Similarly, \( p \) divides \( g \), contradicting \( \gcd(f, g) = 1 \). Hence, \( \gcd(f, g) = 1 \) in \( F[x] \). Additionally, since \( R[x] \) is a UFD, Bezout’s identity \( af + bg = 1 \) for some \( a, b \in R[x] \) holds, and remains valid in \( F[x] \), confirming coprimality there.
