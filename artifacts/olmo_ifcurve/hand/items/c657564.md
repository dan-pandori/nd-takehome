## Answer

Relativistic speed refers to velocities nearing a significant fraction of light speed (c), where classical physics fails 🚀. Einstein’s theory of special relativity governs how objects behave under such extreme conditions 🧠. Time slows down for fast-moving objects compared to stationary ones—this is time dilation ⏳. Objects also contract in length along their direction of motion, called length contraction 📏. As objects approach light speed, the energy needed to accelerate them rises exponentially 💥. Nothing with mass can reach or exceed c—it’s the universe’s cosmic speed limit 🛑. Particle accelerators like the LHC achieve relativistic speeds for subatomic particles 🌌✨. Even GPS satellites must adjust for relativistic effects to work properly 🛰️⚡. Everyday speeds, like cars or planes, are *not* relativistic—light outpaces them by millions of times 🚗💨. Relativity helps us grasp cosmic phenomena, like black holes or the Big Bang 🔭🌌!
