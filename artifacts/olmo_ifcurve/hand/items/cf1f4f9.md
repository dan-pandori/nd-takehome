### **SCENE 2: THE GAS STATION SUMMIT**  
**EXT. “SKYVIEW MINIMART” - SUNSET**  

**NIGHTFALL** has not yet fallen. The flickering neon sign of a dilapidated gas station reads **“SKYVIEW PARKING”**—peeling letters, graffiti-strewn pumps, but Alex steps back, jaw slack.  

**WIDE SHOT:** Behind the gas station, a valley unfolds like a watercolor. The sun dips low, painting the sky in **FIERY ORANGE** and **VELVETY PURPLE**. A lone oak tree sways in a breeze that *isn’t* there—CGI magic? Divine intervention?  

**ALEX**  
*(whispering, arms outstretched)*  
“Is this real? Did I eat too much jerky?”  
*(Explanation: The contrast is surreal—the ugliest gas station on Earth cradles a sunset from heaven. Alex’s disbelief is comedically literal.)*  

A **GAS STATION ATTENDANT (60s)** rocks on a rusty stool. He wears a trucker’s cap and a knowing smile.  

**ATTENDANT**  
“The sunset’s free, sugar. Complimentary with every gallon of regret you pump.”  
*(Explanation: The attendant’s folksy wit highlights the irony—the gas station’s “view” is both metaphor and literal advertisement.)*  

**ALEX**  
*(teary-eyed, pointing at the sky)*  
“This… this is better than *words*. How? Why?!”  
*(Explanation: Alex’s hyperbole is justified—the sky now feels like a gift wrapped in dumpster-fire decor.)*  

**ATTENDANT**

*(Explanation: The attendant’s philosophy is the film’s thesis: beauty and decay are bedfellows.)*  

Alex buys a Slurpee. The drink’s neon-pink hue reflects the sunset. They sip, savoring both.  

**CLOSE-UP:** Alex’s reflection in the gas station’s grimy window. Their face is half-lit by divine orange and half by the attendant’s flickering **“CASH ONLY”** sign.

### **SCENE 3: AFTERMATH**  
**EXT. THE GAS STATION - LATER**  

Alex sits cross-legged on the curb, clutching their Slurpee. The sky has deepened to indigo, starsumbling. The **CROW** from earlier lands beside them, eyeing the drink.  

**ALEX**  
*(to the crow, grinning)*  
“You’re welcome to share. It’s the least I can do.”  
*(Explanation: The crow’s presence ties the two settings—the crow was there at the ugly summit too, just as indifferent. Now, Alex offers peace instead of resentment.)*
