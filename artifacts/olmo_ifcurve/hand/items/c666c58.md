## Answer

This delayed obliteration of the arterial lumen can arise through multiple mechanisms, **or** it may involve a combination of **but** distinct processes. **First**, the roughened or torn intima triggers traumatic thrombosis, as platelets and fibrin aggregate to form a clot that occludes the lumen. **Second**, **or** subintimal dissection occurs when elevated blood flow forces the torn intimal flap to retract into the artery wall, physically blocking distal blood flow. **Moreover**, **even though** the arterial wall’s outer layers remain intact, an intramural hematoma can develop due to medial disruption, gradually compressing the lumen over time. **These mechanisms often coexist**, **and** their effects compound; for instance, thrombosis might stabilize an intimal flap, **while** a growing hematoma exacerbates luminal narrowing. **However**, each pathway’s dominance depends on factors like injury severity, hemodynamic stress, or underlying inflammatory responses, **so** timely intervention is critical to mitigate complete occlusion.
