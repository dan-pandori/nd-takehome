## Answer

Define a field extension \( E/F \) where \( F \subseteq E \) are fields. Let \( \text{Gal}(E/F) = \{ \sigma: E \to E \,|\, \sigma \text{ is an automorphism fixing } F \text{ pointwise} \} \) denote its Galois group. For \( E/F \) to be Galois, it must be normal (every irreducible \( f(x) \in F[x] \) splitting in \( E \)) and separable (all elements in \( E \) have minimal polynomials with distinct roots). The Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory establishes a bijection between subgroups \( H \leq \text{Gal}(E/F) \) and intermediate fields \( K \) with \( F \subseteq K \subseteq E \):  

1. To each subgroup \( H \), assign \( E^H = \{ \alpha \in E \,|\, \sigma(\alpha) = \alpha \, \forall \sigma \in H \} \), its **fixed field**.  
2. To each intermediate field \( K \), assign \( \text{Gal}(E/K) = \{ \sigma \in \text{Gal}(E/F) \,|\, \sigma(\alpha) = \alpha \, \forall \alpha \in K \} \), its **automorphism group**.  

This correspondence is order-reversing: if \( H_1 \subseteq H_2 \), then \( E^{H_2} \subseteq E^{H_1} \), and \([E:E^{H}] = |H|\). For example, if \( E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \) and \( F = \mathbb{Q} \), then \( \text{Gal}(E/F) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \), with subgroups \( \{e\}, \{e, \sigma\} \) fixing fields \( \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \) and \( \mathbb{Q} \), respectively.  

For a separable polynomial \( f(x) \in F[x] \), its splitting field \( E \) is Galois if all roots of \( f \) lie in \( E \). The Galois group \( \text{Gal}(E/F) \) acts transitively on roots, giving \( |\text{Gal}(E/F)| = [E:F] \). In the case \( f(x) = x^3 - 2 \), \( E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega) \) (with \( \omega \) a primitive cube root of unity) has \( [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega):\mathbb{Q}] = 6 \), and \( \text{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3 \). Subgroups like \( A_3 \) correspond to intermediate fields \( \mathbb{Q}(\omega) \), verifying \( |\text{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}(\omega))| = 3 \).  

The **fixed field** \( E^H \) satisfies \( [E^H:F] = [E:F]/[H:\text{Gal}(E/F)] \). For instance, if \( H \) is the stabilizer of an element \( \alpha \in E \), then \( [E:F(\alpha)] = |\text{Stab}(\alpha)| \). This links field extensions to group structure via the Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem.  

Galois theory resolves solvability by radicals: an equation \( f(x) = 0 \) is solvable iff its Galois group is a **solvable group**. The Abel-Ruffini theorem states \( x^5 - 4x + 2 \) has no radical solution because \( S_5 \) is not solvable.  

In summary, the interplay between \( \text{Gal}(E/F) \) and intermediate fields \( K \) is governed by:  
\[
\text{Subgroups } H \ of \ \text{Gal}(E/F) \ \leftrightarrow \ Intermediat\ fields \ E^H, \quad [E:E^H] = |H|,
\]
with the correspondence reflecting algebraic and arithmetic structures intrinsic to field extensions.
