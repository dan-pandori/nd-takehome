Nest parentheses (and [brackets {and braces}]) at least 5 levels deep. Determine the relationship of two lines: Vector r = (-2, 3, 4) + p(6, -2, 3), p∈R Vector r = (-2, 3, -4) + q(6, -2, 11), q∈R

## Answer

To determine the relationship between the two given lines in 3D space:

**First Line:** \(\mathbf{r} = (-2, 3, 4) + p(6, -2, 3)\)  
**Second Line:** \(\mathbf{r} = (-2, 3, -4) + q(6, -2, 11)\)

### Analysis:
1. **Direction Vectors:**
   - First line's direction vector: \((6, -2, 3)\).
   - Second line's direction vector: \((6, -2, 11)\).
   - **Check for Parallelism:**  
     The direction vectors are not scalar multiples because the \(z\)-component ratio \(3/11 \neq 1\) (while \(6/6 = 1\) and \(-2/-2 = 1\)). Thus, the lines are **not parallel**.

2. **Check for Intersection:**
   - Set the parametric equations equal:
     \[
     \begin{cases}
     -2 + 6p = -2 + 6q \\
     3 - 2p = 3 - 2q \\
     4 + 3p = -4 + 11q
     \end{cases}
     \]
   - From the first equation: \(6p = 6q \Rightarrow p = q\).
   - Substitute \(p = q\) into the third equation:
     \[
     4 + 3p = -4 + 11p \Rightarrow 3p - 11p = -8 \Rightarrow -8p = -8 \Rightarrow p = 1 \quad (\text{thus } q = 1)
     \]
   - Verify \(p = 1\) and \(q = 1\) in all equations:
     - First line: \((-2 + 6 \cdot 1, 3 - 2 \cdot 1, 4 + 3 \cdot 1) = (4, 1, 7)\).
     - Second line: \((-2 + 6 \cdot 1, 3 - 2 \cdot 1, -4 + 11 \cdot 1) = (4, 1, 7)\).
   - Both parameters yield the same point \((4, 1, 7)\), so the lines **intersect**.

### Conclusion:
The two lines **intersect at the point \((4, 1, 7)\)**.
