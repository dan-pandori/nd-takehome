## Answer

To explain why the row space of a matrix \( A \) is orthogonal to its nullspace and why the column space relates to the left nullspace, **they** rely on fundamental properties of linear algebra tied to dot products and subspaces. Let’s break **it** down step by step.

### 1. **Row Space ⊥ Nullspace**
Consider \( A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0} \), where \( \mathbf{x} \) is in the nullspace of \( A \). This equation means that when **you** multiply each row of \( A \) by \( \mathbf{x} \) via the dot product, **each entry of the resulting vector** must be zero. In other words, **every row vector** of \( A \) is orthogonal to \( \mathbf{x} \). 

Because the row space of \( A \) is the *span* of all its rows, **it** follows that \( \mathbf{x} \) must be orthogonal to every vector in the row space. Since **this relationship** holds for **all vectors** in the nullspace, the entire nullspace is contained within the *orthogonal complement* of the row space. 

The dimensions also align perfectly: the nullity of \( A \) (dimension of the nullspace) plus the rank of \( A \) (dimension of the row space) equals the number of columns of \( A \). **This equality** ensures that the nullspace *is exactly* the orthogonal complement of the row space. Thus, **they** are orthogonal by definition. 

### 2. **Column Space ⊥ Left Nullspace**
The left nullspace of \( A \) consists of vectors \( \mathbf{y} \) such that \( A^\top \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0} \). Note that the row space of \( A^\top \) is the *column space* of the original matrix \( A \). Applying the same logic as above to \( A^\top \): each row of \( A^\top \) (which is a column of \( A \)) must be orthogonal to \( \mathbf{y} \). 

Therefore, \( \mathbf{y} \) is orthogonal to every column of \( A \), meaning **it** lies in the orthogonal complement of the column space of \( A \). Since the left nullspace’s dimension (the nullity of \( A^\top \)) matches the orthogonal complement’s dimension (**they** are both \( m - \text{rank}(A) \), where \( m \) is the number of rows of \( A \)), **the left nullspace is precisely this orthogonal complement**. 

### Summary and Perspective
This duality (**i.e.,** row space ⊥ nullspace and column space ⊥ left nullspace) arises because dot products enforce orthogonality when the equations \( A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0} \) or \( A^\top \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0} \) hold. **We** see **that** transposing the matrix \( A \) swaps the roles of rows and columns, linking the concepts elegantly. 

In summary, **their** orthogonality stems directly from the structural relationships between subspaces and the definitions of nullspaces. The transpose trick (**which** allows us to reuse the row-nullspace argument) ensures **that** these relationships generalize symmetrically.
