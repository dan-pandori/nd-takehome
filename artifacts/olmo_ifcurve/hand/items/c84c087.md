# Item c84c087

## Prompt (as the model received it)

particle of mass m moves only along a frictionless, vertical, circular track of radius a. (a) Write the classical energy of this mass as a function of angular momentum, neglecting gravity, and assume that the quantum mechanical Hamiltonian comes from substituting the quantum angular momentum operator. What is the Hamiltonian operator? (b) Write Schrodinger equation in the position representation. The angle φ should figure prominently. (c) Find the energy eigenvalues and energy eigenfunctions in the position representation. Use at least 5 different coordinating conjunctions in the response.

## Verifiable constraints

- `count:conjunctions` {"small_n": 5.0}: followed (official strict checker)

## Answer

The classical energy is \( E = \frac{L^2}{2ma^2} \), so the quantum Hamiltonian substitutes \( L \rightarrow -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi} \), yielding \boxed{ H = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial \phi^2} }. The Schrödinger equation in position representation is \boxed{ i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2} \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial \phi^2} }. The energy eigenvalues are \( E_n = \frac{\hbar^2 n^2}{2ma^2} \) where \( n \) is an integer, and the eigenfunctions \boxed{ \psi_n(\phi) \propto e^{in\phi} } must satisfy periodic boundary conditions. But non-integer \( n \) violate this, so quantization is enforced. Nor can fractional values persist for continuity, requiring strict integers. For all \( n \in \mathbb{Z} \), these solutions form an orthonormal basis yet retain exponential form.
