## Answer

* The function \( f \) is Riemann integrable on \([0, 5]\) because it is bounded and has discontinuities only at a finite number of points \(\{1, 2, 3\}\), which form a set of measure zero.  
  - Specifically, \( f \) is piecewise constant, and the discontinuities occur at isolated points where the left and right limits exist finitely, satisfying the criteria for Riemann integrability.  

* The integral of \( f \) over \([0, 5]\) is computed by summing the integrals over each constant interval where \( f \) is defined:  
  - \[ \int_{0}^{1} 1 \, dx + \int_{1}^{2} 3 \, dx + \int_{2}^{3} 2 \, dx + \int_{3}^{5} 4 \, dx \]  
  - Evaluating each term separately:  
    - \( \int_{0}^{1} 1 \, dx = 1 \cdot (1 - 0) = 1 \),  
    - \( \int_{1}^{2} 3 \, dx = 3 \cdot (2 - 1) = 3 \),  
    - \( \int_{2}^{3} 2 \, dx = 2 \cdot (3 - 2) = 2 \),  
    - \( \int_{3}^{5} 4 \, dx = 4 \cdot (5 - 3) = 8 \).  
  - Summing these results: \( 1 + 3 + 2 + 8 = 14 \).  

* The existence of this unique integral value (\( 14 \)) further confirms the Riemann integrability of \( f \), as integrable functions must have a well-defined integral over the interval.
