# Item c507f5a

## Prompt (as the model received it)

Prove the product of two primitive polynomials in a UFD R[x] is primitive. Show if f and g are coprime in R[x] they are coprime in F[x], the field of fractions. The response must start with a verb.

## Verifiable constraints

- `words:start_verb` {}: followed (official strict checker)

## Answer

Prove the product of two primitive polynomials in a UFD \( R[x] \) is primitive by showing no nontrivial common divisor exists in \( R \). Let \( f, g \in R[x] \) be primitive, and let \( h = fg \). Suppose a prime \( p \in R \) divides all coefficients of \( h \). For each coefficient \( c_t = \sum_{i+j=t} a_i b_j \), \( p \) divides \( c_t \). If \( p \) does not divide \( a_0 \), then \( p \) divides \( b_0 \) (from \( c_0 \)). Inductively, for each \( t \), consider \( c_t \): terms with \( i \geq 1 \) involve \( b_{t-i} \) (divisible by \( p \) by induction), leaving \( a_0 b_t \). Since \( p \) divides \( c_t \), it must divide \( b_t \). Thus \( p \) divides all \( b_j \), contradicting \( g \)'s primitiveness. Similarly, if \( p \) divides all \( a_i \), it contradicts \( f \)'s primitiveness. Hence, no such \( p \) exists, so \( h \) is primitive. For coprimality, suppose \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime in \( R[x] \), but assume a nonunit \( d(x) \in F[x] \) divides both. Write \( d(x) = s(x)/t(x) \) with \( s,t \in R[x] \) coprime. Then \( f = d(x)h(x) \) implies \( f \cdot t(x) = s(x)h_1(x) \), so \( s(x) \) divides \( ft \) in \( R[x] \). Since \( s \) and \( t \) are coprime, \( s \) divides \( f \). As \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime, \( s \) must be a unit, and similarly \( t \) is a unit. Thus \( d(x) \) is a unit in \( F[x] \), so \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime in \( F[x] \).
