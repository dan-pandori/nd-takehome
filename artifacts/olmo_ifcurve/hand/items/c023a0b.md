## Answer

To transform \( f(x) = \frac{1}{x^3} \) into \( g(x) = \frac{2}{(x-3)^2} + 1 \), first apply a horizontal shift 3 units to the right by replacing \( x \) with \( x - 3 \), resulting in \( \frac{1}{(x-3)^3} \). Next, the exponent in the denominator is changed from 3 to 2 by raising the shifted function to the power \( \frac{2}{3} \), followed by vertically scaling the result by 2 and shifting upward by 1 unit.  

* A horizontal shift right by 3 units alters the input value to \( x - 3 \), moving the vertical asymptote from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = 3 \).  
* Raising the function to the power \( \frac{2}{3} \), scaling by 2 vertically, and shifting upward by 1 adjusts both the exponent in the denominator and the vertical positioning to match \( g(x) \).
