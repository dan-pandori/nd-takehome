## Answer

The delayed obliteration of the arterial lumen arises through multiple interconnected processes, **for** example: **first**, the roughened intima attracts platelets **and** fibrin, **which** collectively form a traumatic thrombus—a process **but** often amplified by red blood cell entrapment. **Alternatively**, blood flow may dynamically elevate the torn intimal edge, **OR** tear it further, **creating** a subintimal dissection **THAT** physically displaces the flap **and** obstructs the distal lumen. **Yet**, even in cases **where** the arterial coats appear intact, an intramural hematoma can develop between the media **and** adventitia, **blocking** the lumen **by** compressing the remaining blood channels. **Moreover**, these mechanisms rarely act in isolation; they frequently **AND** synergistically contribute **to** the gradual closure, highlighting **how** hemodynamic forces **and** tissue injury **together** drive this complex vascular pathology.
