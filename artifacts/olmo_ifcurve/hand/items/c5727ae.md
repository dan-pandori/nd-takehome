## Answer

### (a) Classical Energy and Hamiltonian Operator  
The classical energy of a mass \( m \) moving along a frictionless vertical circular track of radius \( a \), neglecting gravity, is purely kinetic:  
\[
E_{\text{classical}}(L) = \frac{L^2}{2ma^2},
\]  
where \( L \) is the angular momentum (\( L = m a^2 \omega \), with \( \omega = \dot{\phi} \)).  

When quantized, the angular momentum operator \( L_z \) replaces \( L \). In cylindrical coordinates for a ring (fixed \( r = a \)), \( L_z = -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi} \). Substituting into the classical Hamiltonian yields the **quantum Hamiltonian operator**:  
\[
\hat{H} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial \phi^2}.
\]

---

### (b) Schrödinger Equation in Position Representation  
In the position representation (\( \phi \) as the coordinates), the time-independent Schrödinger equation is:  
\[
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2} \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial \phi^2} = E \psi(\phi).
\]  
This second-order differential equation must be solved with **boundary conditions** reflecting the periodicity of \( \phi \):  
\[
\psi(\phi + 2\pi) = \psi(\phi),
\]  
since the track is a closed loop.

---

### (c) Energy Eigenvalues and Eigenfunctions  
**Or** begin by recognizing the solutions to the Schrödinger equation:  
\[
\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial \phi^2} = -\frac{2ma^2E}{\hbar^2} \psi(\phi),
\]  
possess sinusoidal forms. **For** real energy eigenvalues \( E \), define \( k^2 = \frac{2ma^2E}{\hbar^2} \), leading to the general solution:  
\[
\psi(\phi) = A e^{i k \phi} + B e^{-i k \phi}.
\]  

**But** periodicity demands \( \psi(\phi + 2\pi) = \psi(\phi) \). Applying **this** condition:  
\[
e^{i k (2\pi)} = 1 \quad \implies \quad k = n,
\]  
**so** the wave number \( k \) must be an integer (\( n \in \mathbb{Z} \)). Thus, the energy eigenvalues are quantized:  
\[
E_n = \frac{\hbar^2 n^2}{2ma^2}, \quad n = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \dots
\]  

**Moreover**, the eigenfunctions are:  
\[
\psi_n(\phi) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{i n \phi},
\]  
normalized over \( \phi \in [0, 2\pi) \). These functions form an orthonormal basis **nor** can they be expressed as real combinations (via sine/cosine) **though** maintaining the same energies.  

**Finally**, the **yet** degenerate states \( +n \) and \( -n \) share \( E_n \) but differ in \( \phi \)-dependence. The mathematical structure **hence** reflects quantized angular momentum, with quantization arising **specifically** from the ring’s topology.
