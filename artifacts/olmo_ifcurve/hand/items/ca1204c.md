# Item ca1204c

## Prompt (as the model received it)

Prove the product of two primitive polynomials in a UFD R[x] is primitive. Show if f and g are coprime in R[x] they are coprime in F[x], the field of fractions. The response must start with a verb.

- `words:start_verb` {}: followed (official strict checker)

## Answer

Prove that the product \( f \cdot g \) of two primitive polynomials in a UFD \( R[x] \) is primitive. Let \( f, g \in R[x] \) be primitive, so the gcd of their coefficients is 1. Assume \( d \neq 1 \) divides all coefficients of \( f \cdot g \). Since \( R \) is a UFD, \( d \) factors into irreducibles. Let \( p \) be an irreducible dividing \( d \). In the ring \( S = R/(p) \), which is an integral domain, \( f \cdot g \equiv 0 \). Thus, \( f \equiv 0 \) or \( g \equiv 0 \) in \( S \). However, this contradicts primitiveness of \( f \) or \( g \), since \( p \) would divide all coefficients. Hence, \( d = 1 \), proving \( f \cdot g \) is primitive.

Show that if \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime in \( R[x] \), they are coprime in \( F[x] \), where \( F \) is the field of fractions of \( R \). Suppose \( d(x) \in F[x] \) divides both \( f \) and \( g \). Clear denominators by multiplying \( d(x) \) by a common denominator \( a \in R \), yielding \( a \cdot d(x) \in R[x] \). Since \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime in \( R[x] \), \( a \cdot d(x) \) must be a unit in \( R[x] \). Thus, \( d(x) \) is a unit in \( F[x] \). Therefore, no nontrivial common divisors exist in \( F[x] \), so \( f \) and \( g \) are coprime there.
