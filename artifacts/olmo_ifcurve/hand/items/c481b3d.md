## Answer

The irreversible alteration of Earth's biophysical systems, driven by anthropogenic activities, has prompted the formalization of the "Anthropocene" as a geological epoch (Witze, 2024; Moore, 2016). This neologism, initially proposed by Stoermer and Crutzen in 2000 (Nixon, 2011), arose from interdisciplinary observations of profound ecological and geomorphological changes since the industrial era. Scholars such as Smith and Zeder (2013) advocate for its adoption into stratigraphic classification, arguing that the Anthropocene epoch signifies a paradigm shift from the Holocene, characterized by humanity's unprecedented biophysical influence on global systems. Haraway (2016) further emphasizes this conceptual reframing by challenging traditional dichotomies between human and natural realms, positioning the Anthropocene as a testament to anthropogenic-driven transformations in biodiversity, climate patterns, and sedimentary records. This interdisciplinary consensus underscores the urgency of recognizing humanity's geological agency and necessitates collaborative, evidence-based approaches to mitigate escalating planetary thresholds and redefine sustainable trajectories for Earth's future.
