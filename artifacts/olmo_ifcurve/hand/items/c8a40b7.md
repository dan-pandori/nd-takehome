## Answer

Can I leave him, this spindly patriarch adrift in the dusk of his own making, when the coins clink in my pocket like a fortune teller’s warning, **and** the wind whispers of distant lands where the soul might fly unshackled, **but** the shadow of duty clings like morning mildew, **or** is it fear, that slick serpent, coiling through my marrow, that stays my hand? The old man’s breath, a ragged psalm, knots the air—his eyes, twin voids, sip the last dregs of a life once thick as ale. What monstrous choice looms here? To flee would be to cast him adrift on a sea of Want, his substantiate shadow gnawing at the edges of memory, while I, lithe as a hound, slink off to nibble on bones of wanderlust. Yet, to stay… ah, the stench of obligation, the rot of unspoken guilt! The floorboards creak: *Is it not I who dreams in turns of both flight and chains?* The night, that old sly fox, grins through cracks in the wall, and the question hums—a vagrant tune between two worlds, a duet of hunger and conscience, forever unresolved, like the ache between nightfall and first light.
