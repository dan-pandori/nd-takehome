## Answer

The delayed obliteration of an arterial lumen can occur through various mechanisms, **and** one involves a damaged intima attracting platelets and fibrin, which form a traumatic thrombus. **Alternatively**, when blood flow exerts force on the torn intima, it may create a subintimal dissection **or** lift an intimal flap that obstructs the lumen. **Yet** even when the artery’s inner and outer coats remain intact, an intramural hematoma may develop, **so** the lumen is ultimately blocked by this growing pressure or mass. These processes highlight how structural disruptions, hemodynamic forces, and internal bleeding can collectively lead to arterial occlusion over time.
