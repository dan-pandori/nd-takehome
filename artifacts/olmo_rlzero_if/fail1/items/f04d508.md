Details

Word Error Rate (WER) is a metric widely used in evaluating the performance of Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems. The formula for calculating WER is as follows:

WER = \frac{D + S + I}{N} * 100%

Where:

- D = Number of deleted words
- S = Number of substituted words
- I = Number of inserted words
- N = Number of words in the reference (ground truth) utterance

WER focuses on identifying discrepancies between the transcribed text and the actual spoken words. Here's a breakdown of the three types of errors it measures:

- **Substitution (S):** When a word in the input speech is transcribed as a different word.
- **Insertion (I):** When an extra word is inserted in the transcription that wasn't in the original speech.
- **Deletion (D):** When a word in the original speech is missed and not included in the transcription.

Therefore, the three types of recognition errors **WER does not typically measure** are:
  
- **Negation (C):** In the context of speech recognition, negation refers to a modifier that transforms the propositions it modifies, often changing the truth value of a sentence. The focus of WER is on the fidelity of the transcription to the original speech, not on the logical meaning or truth value of the proposition being affected by negation. While negations can be contextually significant, they are not considered direct 'errors' in the sense of incorrect transcribing of words. Instead, they are part of the more complex task of understanding and interpreting natural language, which goes beyond the scope of WER. 

In summary, the WER is concerned primarily with the accuracy of word-level transcriptions without considering the semantic impact of words like negations in transforming the meaning of the utterance.
