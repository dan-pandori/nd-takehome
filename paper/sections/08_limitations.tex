\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scale and domain.} One architecture with 9.56M parameters, propositional logic over at most four atoms,
and pretraining proofs of at most 12 lines. Nothing here is measured on a large language model or on first-order logic.
The organism was chosen because it allows interventions that large models do not, and its results are results about
it.
\item \textbf{One RL algorithm, 16 rounds, one composition.} Expert iteration at $k=32$, $T=0.8$ is rejection-sampling
fine-tuning. GRPO-style updates, more exploration, demonstrations or longer runs could behave differently, and in the
closest controlled study and in our earlier organism GRPO did (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). One knockout ladder was still
changing at round 24. Only one composition was removed.
\item \textbf{What the headline test measures.} The pre-registered indicator is largely decided before RL. The RL-specific
contrasts (gains during RL, families acquired during RL) were not pre-registered, and on the three full seeds that
match the knockout's hardware the during-RL contrast is 2/3 vs 0/6 ($p = 0.034$).
\item \textbf{Seeds and power.} Six seeds per main arm power the primary indicator but not most per-family tests (three
families 3 vs 0, Holm 0.17; excluded middle 1 vs 0). A 0/6 outcome bounds the per-seed rate of acquisition from a
\sig-free base below 0.39 only. The dose arm has three seeds and two doses (0\% and 1\%) cannot locate a threshold.
\item \textbf{The knockout is not surgical, and exposure includes replay.} It makes \texttt{DN} and $\neg$-introduction
three to four times rarer and removes two thirds of the classical-only theorems. It costs a few theorems of general
ability (\S\ref{sec:side}). Exposure in pretraining cannot be separated from exposure through replay during RL.
\item \textbf{``Absent'' is not established.} Five of six knockout bases proved strict theorems with \sig{} at large
$k$. Only one is certified out of reach as pre-registered, on a pooled mean rate, at one allocation, with an 8\% margin.
No family could be certified at the sampling used, so the \textsc{created} verdict was unreachable by design.
\item \textbf{Within-reach labels.} Per-theorem base rates in the knockout arm rest on one to three successes in fewer
than $K$ attempts, from sampling that paused after a first success.
\item \textbf{Estimators.} The known-proof estimate runs two- to three-fold low below $10^{-4}$ and was calibrated on
full-exposure bases. The likelihood scores use the full ladders' proofs and one line-numbering offset.
\item \textbf{Budgets.} $K$ depends on the allocation, on the number of evaluation theorems it is spread over, and on
one measured GPU-class ratio. The knockout certificates and \dcov{} were computed at one allocation.
\item \textbf{Settings.} Old and new full seeds differ in GPU class, batch, resumption and evaluation-phase sampling,
and are pooled. Many evaluations cut off more than 0.1\% of attempts in some stratum (up to 8.8\% of strict-theorem
attempts on one dose seed); see Appendix~\ref{sec:checks}. The power-sampling comparators kept no proof text, so
their few successes were not re-checked in Lean.
\item \textbf{Interface.} In an earlier 3.2M organism, a base acting through the proof-state interface reached most of
the theorems that whole-proof EI had ``created'', with two seeds per arm and the state confounded with step-by-step
generation. Whether creation verdicts depend on the interface is untested at this scale.
\end{itemize}
