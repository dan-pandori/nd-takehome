\section{Results: the \sig{} knockout}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers in this section are on the 9.56M-parameter models of Table~\ref{tab:arms}, judged by Lean alone. Seeds are
listed in order s0, s1, \dots{} Unless stated, a count is from the plain read with sample seed x0.

\subsection{What the knockout removes, and what else it changes}
\label{sec:ko-data}

\KTko{} differs from \KT{} in exactly the 16,961 \sigp{} records, and the refill matches the removal length by length.
The knockout ladders' replay never carried a \sig{} or \sigp{} record and never leaked a removed record. This held in
every round's fine-tuning mix, on all six seeds. Every \sig{} proof a knockout model trained on was its own RL proof.

Removing a composition also changes the data around it. In \KTko{}, \texttt{DN} appears in 3.29\% of records (13.73\%
in \KT{}) and $\neg$-introduction in 5.19\% (15.21\%). Classically valid but intuitionistically unprovable theorems fall
from 4.17\% to 1.35\% of a 20,000-record sample. The remainder use \texttt{DN} on double negations that a premise or an
assumption supplies. Mean proof term size moves from 35.91 to 36.25 nodes. On general ability, the knockout bases are
6.88 points worse at greedy decoding of 5,000 held-out generator theorems (0.8829 vs 0.9517, means over seeds). The
gap comes from the 679 theorems whose reference proof uses \sig{} (0.45 vs 0.96). On the other 4,321 the knockout
is 0.12 points better. With 256 attempts, though, the knockout bases also solve fewer intuitionistic theorems. They
solve 4.3 fewer of holdout250's 225 non-classical theorems, and 4.0 fewer of textbook72's 53 intuitionistic theorems.
The textbook72 comparison rests on three full seeds, s3--s5, against six knockout seeds. By r16 the holdout250 gap has closed (0.996 vs 0.996 on its
non-classical theorems), but textbook72's has not ($-2.5$ theorems on its non-classical members). The knockout is
therefore not perfectly surgical. We return to this in \S\ref{sec:limits}.

At r16, every full-exposure seed has at least one classical family at the bar, and no knockout seed has one: 6/6
against 0/6, one-sided Boschloo $p = 0.00024$ (Table~\ref{tab:primary}, Fig.~\ref{fig:endpoint}). The redraw (x1) and
the guided read give the same indicator. So does the pre-registered, looser definition of the held-out sets (178
members), which a pre-registered amendment replaced with the strict sets after the first ladder round. Full seeds
solve 23--77 of the 106 strict members and knockout seeds 0--2 (exact one-sided Mann--Whitney $U = 36$, $p = 0.0011$).
The interquartile means are 53.75 [30.5, 73.5] and 0.75 [0, 1.75], a difference of 53.0 [31.0, 72.8] members (95\%
percentile bootstrap over seeds). The pre-registered verdict is \emph{needs exposure}.

Per family, with Holm correction over six families, converse contraposition earns ``needs exposure'' on its own (6 vs
0 seeds; $p = 0.0002$, Holm 0.0015). Negated conditional, Peirce and the Peirce sequent are each 3 vs 0 ($p = 0.034$,
Holm 0.17): undetermined at six seeds. Excluded middle is 1 vs 0 ($p = 0.39$), and the De Morgan family is 0 vs 0,
since no model solves any of its four strict members.

\paragraph{The RL-only reading.} Converse contraposition is already at the bar at pend on 5/6 full seeds (6/6 in the
guided read). So the primary indicator is partly decided before RL starts. The cleaner statement about RL counts
families that reach the bar at r16 but were not there at pend: 5/6 full seeds against 0/6 knockout seeds
(Boschloo $p = 0.0032$; this reading was not pre-registered). The knockout ladders' training data tell the same story.
Every training target received 32 attempts per round. The knockout ladders solved none of the 123 strict training
targets in round 1, and by round 16 0/5/1/0/0/2 had been solved at least once, cumulatively. The full ladders went
from 21/1/27/17/15/20 to 49/80/86/78/33/36 (Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}a). No knockout ladder ever found proofs for half of any
family's strict training targets.

\subsection{One percent is enough}
\label{sec:dose}

The dose arm pretrains on \KTko{} with 1,550 of the removed \sig{} records restored, 1.00\% of the data. Its three seeds
solve 6/18/8 strict held-out members at pend, 53/33/28 at r8 and 58/39/30 at r16. All three acquire families during RL
(Table~\ref{tab:primary}): 3/3 against the knockout's 0/6, Boschloo $p = 0.0033$ (exploratory). At r16 the dose seeds
sit inside the full arm's range (two-sided Mann--Whitney $p = 0.55$; with three seeds, this is weak evidence of
equality). In round 1, the dose bases wrote 223/183/241 \sig{} proofs on training theorems. The new full seeds wrote
635--885 and the knockout bases 6--13. The dose bases wrote the free positive reductio from round 1. Exposure at 1\% is
thus enough for EI to find and amplify the composition. We did not test lower doses; a 0.1\% set is built but unused.

\subsection{\sig{} is rare in the knockout arm, not absent}
\label{sec:rare}

The knockout bases are not \sig-free in behaviour, and the distinction matters for what the null result means.

\paragraph{In the ladders.} Every knockout ladder wrote \sig{} and \sigp{} proofs in round 1, 6--13 distinct \sig{} proofs
per ladder. They wrote them on theorems whose sequent already supplies $\neg\neg G$ or whose goal is a negation. The
\emph{free positive reductio} (\texttt{DN} on a derived line whose $\neg\neg G$ the theorem does not contain, for a
positive goal $G$) is the genuinely classical step. Every proof of a strict member that uses \sig{} uses it in this
form. Over 16 rounds the knockout ladders produced 0/34/4/1/2/16 distinct accepted proofs of this kind, first in rounds
never/9/12/15/15/2. The full ladders produced 17,507--50,222 each, and the dose ladders 20,597--26,662, from round 1
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}b). The lemma route, absent from all pretraining sets, also appears sporadically in knockout
ladders, at 0--2 distinct proofs per round.

\paragraph{In the bases, at large $k$.} We sampled each knockout base about $1.1$--$1.5$ million times, pooled over the
105 strict family members, at its own RL budget $\Ktrain(16)$ (17,189--23,322 attempts per theorem;
Table~\ref{tab:certs}). Two bases (s0, s3) had no success and are certified out of reach. Four wrote \sig{} proofs of
family members 1--6 times. All 14 base successes on strict members are genuine \sig{} proofs: 11 on converse
contraposition, one on the Peirce sequent, and two on the generated theorem. Per member, $\hat p$ is about $10^{-6}$ to
$3\times10^{-4}$. Knockout seed 1's base has 4 of its 18 strict converse-contraposition members within reach at its
budget ($\hat p = 1.0$--$3.3\times10^{-4} \geq 1/17{,}189$). No family is certified out of reach for any seed: the
largest $K$ certifiable per family is below RL's budget.

\paragraph{In likelihood.} We scored the best known \sig{} proof of each of 101 strict held-out members under each
model, teacher-forced at $T=0.8$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:logp}). Under the knockout bases the median log-probability is $-26$ to
$-36$ nats; under full seed 0's base it is $-19.2$. The step that costs most is opening the box that assumes $\neg G$:
$-18.0$ to $-21.6$ nats under the knockout bases, against $-10.5$ under the full base. Along the ladders, RL moves the
full and dose models toward these proofs: from $-15.4$ to $-5.1$ nats for full s3, and from $-25.3$ to $-8.7$ for dose
s0. It does not move the knockout models. Their medians at r16 are no closer. Four of six fall, and the largest rise is 3.7 nats (s0),
against 10.3 nats for full s3 and 16.6 for dose s0. Two caveats apply. The scores use one naming offset, so they understate log-probability by at most
$\ln 33 \approx 3.5$ nats. And the scored proofs are the full ladders' proofs, so a knockout model that found its own
spelling (seed 1, below) need not raise them.

\paragraph{The base's prior predicts what RL acquires (exploratory).} Across the 13 ladders with a scored base, the
base's median log-probability of known \sig{} proofs is rank-correlated with strict members solved at r16 (Spearman
$\rho = 0.88$, Fig.~\ref{fig:prior}). That is mostly the difference between arms ($\rho = 0.39$ without the knockout).
Within a ladder, the base's log-probability of a member's best known proof predicts whether the member is solved at r16.
The AUC is 0.92--0.95 on the four full seeds with scored bases, against a pre-registered bar of 0.7. It is
0.85--0.86 on the three dose seeds. Comparing only members of the same family, the AUC is 0.47--0.98 (full) and
0.28--0.60 (dose). It also predicts \emph{when} a member is first solved in the ladder's per-round samples of held-out
theorems. Spearman $\rho$ between the base log-probability and the first-solve round is $-0.69$ to $-0.87$ on the full
seeds and $-0.61$ to $-0.72$ on the dose seeds (unsolved members counted as round 17). Within families it averages
$-0.33$ to $-0.48$ on the full seeds, against $-0.22$ to $+0.14$ on the dose seeds. The base's prior thus predicts
which families RL acquires, and on the full seeds it also predicts the order within a family.

\subsection{The one knockout ladder that moved}
\label{sec:ko-s1}

Knockout seed 1 was the only ladder whose free reductio kept spreading, so we continued it to round 24 (exploratory).
Its free positive reductio proofs per round grew from 11 in round 16 to 123 in round 24. Its share of converse
contraposition among strict training targets reached 0.33 from round 21. But its strict training targets stalled at 9,
and the plain read solves the \emph{same five} strict held-out members at r20 and r24: four converse-contraposition
members and the generated theorem. It solves them more often. One member goes from 0 to 32 to 214 successes per 256
attempts at r16, r20 and r24. Three of its four converse-contraposition members (\texttt{la\_transfer\_1246},
\texttt{1460}, \texttt{2256}) are among the four its own base already had within reach at RL's budget. No family
reaches the bar. We read this as RL eliciting a rare behaviour of this particular base, not creating a capability. Its
r20 proofs of these members take three routes: \sig{} (32 proofs), other \sigp{} (24) and the lemma route (7).

\subsection{What did not help, and what the knockout does not change}
\label{sec:nulls}

\paragraph{Seeding one proof.} From each knockout r16 checkpoint we ran one more EI round, with and without four copies
of knockout seed 1's base's own free-\sig{} proof of the generated theorem. Seeded minus control was $+0/+0/+2/+0/+0$
strict family members on seeds s0, s1, s2, s3, s5 (s4 was not run). Seed 2's two were single successes in 256 attempts
(one-sided exact $p=0.25$). No family reached the bar. One extra round with a single seed proof is not enough.

\paragraph{Theorem-level reach.} On the 322 evaluation theorems, RL's gain over what its own base reaches at RL's
budget is similar in both arms. Each new base received 512 attempts, then at least $\Ktrain(16)$ attempts on every
theorem it had failed. The gain \dcov{} is $+37/+36/+32$ for full seeds s3--s5 and $+29/+31/+35/+37/+26/+21$ for the
knockout seeds. RL's set-level advantage over its base does not depend on \sig{}. These sets are not netted against
RL-free controls.

\paragraph{A sharpening null for one sampler.} Sharpening the base with no training is the strongest alternative to RL
\citep{karan2025power}. We implemented Metropolis--Hastings power sampling: target $p^\alpha$ at the sequence level,
suffix resampling, 32 chains per theorem, blocks of 4 steps, 8 MH steps per block. Each theorem's plain-sampling
comparator got exactly the same number of decoded tokens. On full seeds s0 and s1, the theorems RL r8 solves that the
base cannot reach at $\Ktrain$ were 33 and 27. Power sampling at $\alpha = 4$ and $\alpha=2$ solved 0/33 and 0/27.
Plain sampling solved 1/33 and 1/27 at $T=0.8$, and 1/33 and 2/27 at $T=1.0$. On the knockout bases (s0, s1) power
sampling solved 0/106 strict members, and plain sampling at $T=1.0$ solved 1/106. The sampler fails its own positive
control, however. On 17 theorems the base solves rarely, it solves 0/17 at $\alpha=4$ and at $\alpha=1.5$, where plain
sampling at the same tokens solves 6/17 ($T=0.8$) and 13/17 ($T=1.0$). The power runs also took 1.6--2.7 times the
plain runs' wall-clock. The result is a null for this sampler at these settings, not for sharpening in general. That
fits the finding that MCMC power samplers do not reach $p^\alpha$ at realistic budgets \citep{talwar2026power}.

\subsection{Robustness and checks}
\label{sec:robust}

\paragraph{Lean.} An independent reviewer re-checked with Lean 4.34.1 (the version used in the loop): every stored
knockout proof of a strict member (448/448 accepted), samples from every arm, and all 16,961 refill records. The
negative controls behaved as they should. None of 150 of the reads' own Lean rejections passed, none of 60 proofs under
another theorem, none of 40 strict knockout proofs with \texttt{Classical.byContradiction} removed, and \texttt{sorry}
failed. The pre-filter decided every rejection in knockout seed 1's 2.35 million distinct texts. Re-checking 1,950
pre-filter rejections (150 for each of 13 reasons) in Lean gave 0 accepted, a 95\% upper bound of about 0.15\% on the
false-reject rate. Training never read an evaluation file. The training theorems share no renaming class with any
evaluation set this paper reads, and none of the three pretraining sets shares one with the 106 strict members.
Inherited from \KT{}, the pretraining sets do share a few classes with general-ability sets, under a
premise-order-invariant key: one textbook72 theorem, and 21--22 of the 5,000 held-out greedy theorems.

\paragraph{Cut-offs.} An attempt is cut off when one step exceeds 512 tokens or the proof exceeds 96 steps. On the
strict members at r16, 0.04--4.97\% of knockout attempts were cut off, 0.11--2.89\% of full attempts and 0.68--8.84\% of
dose attempts. A cut-off can only hide a success. The guided reads agree with the plain reads on every knockout and full seed.
They cross the 0.1\% cut-off line in 4 of 27 reads, against 64 of 74 plain reads. This paper adds a paired re-read with doubled caps (\S\ref{sec:checks}).
