\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Does RL expand what the base can do?} The standard test compares pass@$k$ at equal $k$. \citet{yue2025doesrl}
find that base models overtake their RLVR descendants as $k$ grows (to 1,024) and that problems solved by RL but not by
the base are rare. ProRL \citep{liu2025prorl} reports gains where base models fail at any number of attempts, but reads
the base at $k=128$ on tasks where it also struggles with the output format. Much of RL's measured gain corresponds to
operating points the base reaches with search at small sample counts \citep{sun2026budgeted}. Many visible pass@$k$
crossovers are not statistically established \citep{yang2026kernel}. Reward and contamination controls change the
conclusions for pretrained LLMs whose training data are unknown \citep{shao2025spurious,wu2025contamination}. We keep the
question and change the instrument. Lean judges every model, the base's budget is matched to RL's compute and reported
as a curve, and likelihoods bracket the base's per-theorem success rate.

\paragraph{Controlled organisms.} Designs that control pretraining and then intervene on it come closest to separating
creation from elicitation. The nearest to ours is \citet{abdulsalam2026rewrite}. They pretrain a transformer from
scratch on primitive rewrites that exclude later shortcuts. Their base scores 0\% on the two hardest buckets at
pass@1024 while RL solves them at pass@16. Rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT), the family our expert iteration belongs
to, ``then plateaus, while RL improves more slowly at first but continues climbing''. Their outcome is the opposite of
ours, and the designs differ in four ways:
\begin{itemize}
\item their new behaviour is a new action type, while \sig{} is a composition of two rules the base already uses;
\item their creating algorithm is GRPO, while their RFT plateaus;
\item they compare at equal $k$, without likelihoods;
\item they use 16 problems per bucket and three seeds.
\end{itemize}
\citet{tsilivis2025rlntp} set the share of long parity demonstrations in pretraining exactly. They show that RL after
next-token training amplifies that mode, efficiently unless the share is exponentially small; the lowest share in the
notes we verified is 0.01. Interplay \citep{zhang2025interplay} trains a 100M model from scratch on synthetic dependency
graphs and finds that RL transfers to a new context only if the context made up at least 1\% of pretraining, not at 0\%
or 0.1\%. Our 1\% dose result parallels theirs, for an inference composition rather than a context. Other organisms
show policy gradient finding graph paths absent from its fine-tuning data \citep{wang2025rlplanning} and RL surfacing
chess moves to which supervised fine-tuning gave probability below 0.05 \citep{shen2026chess}; under our budgets the
latter would count as elicited. In a single-layer theory, outcome-reward policy gradient becomes infeasible when the
data's mass on simple examples vanishes \citep{ranmilo2026outcomerl}. Knockouts at the fine-tuning or RL stage disagree:
removing a strategy before GRPO leaves only the remaining strategy improved \citep{krishnamurthy2026select}, while a
pattern family removed from RL data still appears after GRPO \citep{park2025countdown}. Neither measures the removed
behaviour's base likelihood. Our contribution to this line is a single-composition knockout in pretraining, with a
matched refill, a dose arm, base rates measured at $10^6$ attempts, and likelihoods of arbitrary proofs at every
checkpoint.

\paragraph{Sharpening without training.} If RL only sharpens the base, an RL-free sharpener should match it.
\citet{karan2025power} sample the base's power distribution $p^\alpha$ by Metropolis--Hastings and approach GRPO on
MATH500 (0.748 vs 0.785) at 8.84 times the tokens. A token-level approximation is reported to match one-shot GRPO at
much lower latency \citep{ji2026scalablepower}; we have read only its abstract. Two later papers complicate a
sharpening null. Practical MCMC and SMC power samplers do not converge to $p^\alpha$ at realistic budgets and behave as
likelihood-guided search \citep{talwar2026power}. And one $\alpha$ sharpens different problems by very different amounts
\citep{yang2026correctmass}. With a binary reward, the KL-regularised RL optimum is the base conditioned on success
\citep{korbak2022rlkl}, which preserves the base's odds among correct outputs. Our power-sampling null fails its own
positive control, so we report it as specific to our sampler.

\paragraph{Elicitation and latent capability.} Safety evaluation defines capability relative to elicitation: a latent
capability is one a model shows with low probability by default that small changes reveal
\citep{hofstatter2025elicitation,vanderweij2024sandbagging}. Password-locked models show that a few demonstrations, or
expert iteration from lucky samples, recover deliberately hidden skills, while RL from a weak policy can fail to explore
\citep{greenblatt2024passwordlocked}. Fine-tuning often enhances or wraps existing mechanisms
\citep{prakash2024finetuning,jain2023mechanistically}, and RL-trained reasoning models orchestrate mechanisms present in
their bases \citep{venhoff2025base}. \citet{li2026elicitcreate} define creation as a change in the ``accessible support''
a model reaches under a stated budget, decoding policy and signal, the conceptual frame closest to our budget-relative
definitions. The strongest ``it was already there'' arguments compare against a control that never had the capability
\citep{deeb2024unlearning,jain2023mechanistically}. Our knockout bases are such a control for one composition, with the
caveat that they still produce it rarely.

\paragraph{Measuring rare behaviour.} With enough samples a model with full support covers everything
\citep{brown2024monkeys}, so per-problem success probabilities and their distribution are the quantities of interest
\citep{schaeffer2025powerlaws,kazdan2025passk}. Sampling resolves probabilities only down to about $1/n$. Sampling until a
fixed number of successes \citep{hu2023passuntil}, importance sampling and tail extrapolation
\citep{wu2024rareoutputs,jones2025forecasting} reach rarer events. Likelihoods of reference solutions predict pass@$k$
\citep{schaeffer2025generative}. Zero successes in $k$ attempts bound $p$ through $(1-p)^k$ \citep{wu2025invisibleleash},
the logic of our certificates. Compute-equivalent gains \citep{davidson2023retraining} and item response theory
\citep{polo2024tinybenchmarks,truong2026irsl} give other common scales. Because Lean checks the whole proof, our pass@$k$
cannot count a lucky guess.

\paragraph{Expert iteration and RL for theorem proving.} Our loop is expert iteration \citep{anthony2017exit} in its
simplest form: sample, keep what the verifier accepts, fine-tune. Neural provers run the same loop with best-first or
tree search as the expert
\citep{polu2020gptf,polu2022curriculum,lample2022htps,wu2024internlmstepprover,xin2025bfsprover}, or use GRPO with a
Lean reward \citep{xin2024deepseekprover}. The loop stalls when hard targets yield no successes. A from-scratch HOL
prover stays at 7\% without injected exploration \citep{bansal2019deepholzero}, and a self-play prover saw 131 correct
proofs in 2.5 million attempts on unproved statements \citep{dong2025stp}. At least one formal-mathematics creation
claim rests on a small base budget \citep{burton2026diversity}. AlphaZero's new chess concepts
\citep{silver2017alphazero,schut2023alphazeroconcepts} are new relative to human play, from random initialisation, not
relative to a pretrained base.
