# Screening agent C: the create-vs-elicit literature and how its verdicts depend on RL scale

Run `rl-compute-share`. Written 2026-10-19 by agent:claude.

**How to read this file**
- Every value has a claim id in `ledger/C_threads.tsv`, and each id holds a verbatim quote. `verify_ledger.py` finds all quotes in their sources.
- "n.r." means not reported (I searched the fetched text and found nothing).
- "Interp." marks my interpretation. Everything else is what the source states.
- I computed no FLOP ratios. Ratios appear only where a source states them.
- Yue et al., SimpleRL-Zoo, DAPO, Oat-Zero, DeepSeek-R1, ScaleRL and the open recipes belong to other agents. They appear here only where a paper below evaluates their checkpoints.

**Shared base-model facts**
- DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B is fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B [C-BASE-r1d15_base]. It was trained for 2–3 epochs [C-BASE-r1d_epochs] on 800,000 samples generated by DeepSeek-R1 [C-BASE-r1d_800k], and DeepSeek-R1 is itself RL-trained [C-BASE-r1d_distilled].
- Qwen2.5-Math was continued-pretrained on Qwen Math Corpus v2 (more than 1T tokens) [C-BASE-q25math_corpus], starting from Qwen2.5 base models [C-BASE-q25math_init] that saw 18T tokens [C-BASE-q25_18T]. The corpus contains synthetic data generated by Qwen2-Math-72B-Instruct [C-BASE-q25math_synth]. Context is 4K [C-BASE-q25math_ctx].
- Qwen3 was pretrained on about 36T tokens [C-BASE-q3_36T]. Its small models (0.6B–14B, including 4B) were post-trained by strong-to-weak distillation [C-BASE-q3_distill] from flagships that had reasoning RL [C-BASE-q3_rl].
- Llama 3.1 was pretrained on about 15T tokens, and its instruct fine-tuning used more than 25M synthetic examples [C-BASE-l31_15T, C-BASE-l31_synth]. Llama 3.2 1B/3B was pretrained on up to 9T tokens [C-BASE-l32_9T] using logits from Llama 3.1 8B/70B [C-BASE-l32_logits].

The paper's own scale argument: earlier elicit-only papers stopped "before models can fully explore", "typically no more than hundreds of steps" [C-PRORL-prior_limits].

**ProRL v2** (NVIDIA Technical Blog, Aug 2025, grade A; HF model card, grade B):
- 3,000 steps at 8K context [C-PRORL2-v2steps_blog, C-PRORL2-steps_mc], with 512 prompts per step per BroRL's baseline row [C-BRORL-baseline_row].
- PPO-Clip with REINFORCE++ baseline, clip-higher, dynamic sampling and a scheduled cosine length penalty [C-PRORL2-algo_blog]. The reference is reset every 200–500 steps without clearing the optimizer [C-PRORL2-reset_blog].
- Verdict (lab claim): pass@1 and pass@k metrics "climb over thousands of RL steps". The blog text gives no k and no numbers [C-PRORL2-verdict_blog, C-PRORL2-moresteps_blog]. Novelty is measured as n-gram overlap with pretraining data [C-PRORL2-ngram_blog].
- A ProRL V2.1 update dated 2026-02-09 exists; its body could not be retrieved [C-PRORL2-v21_notion].
- Revision trap (HF card): v2 was released on 2025-07-23 under the same repo id, and the original v1 needs `revision="v1"` [C-PRORL2-v2_release_mc, C-PRORL2-v1_revision_mc]. Papers that do not state a revision may have evaluated either version.

## 10. Spurious Rewards (Shao et al.): arXiv 2506.10947v2, and one-shot RLVR (Wang et al.): arXiv 2504.20571v3

- **Spurious Rewards.**
  - Training: 300 steps [C-SPUR-steps] on DeepScaleR data [C-SPUR-data]; 64 prompts × 16 rollouts [C-SPUR-config]; lr 5e-7 on 8 GPUs [C-SPUR-lr]; no KL [C-SPUR-kl]; about 24 h on 8 A100 per run [C-SPUR-gpu]; metrics pass@1 and avg@8 only [C-SPUR-eval].
  - Result: Qwen2.5-Math-7B gains +21.4 on MATH-500 with random rewards vs +29.1 with ground truth [C-SPUR-headline]. Spurious rewards fail for Llama3 and OLMo2 [C-SPUR-family]. GRPO clipping bias amplifies pretraining priors [C-SPUR-clip].
  - Verdict: **elicit, explicitly scoped to compute**: "at least at the compute scales of open-source post-training pipelines" [C-SPUR-verdict].
- **One-shot RLVR.**
  - Result: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B goes from 36.0% to 73.6% on MATH500 from one example [C-1SHOT-headline].
  - Training: batch 128 (the example duplicated) × 8 responses, max 3072, KL 1e-3 [C-1SHOT-batch, C-1SHOT-config, C-1SHOT-maxlen, C-1SHOT-kl]. Test gains continue from step 100 to step 1540, and the single example is overfitted only after 1400 / 1800 steps [C-1SHOT-steps, C-1SHOT-overfit]. 8 A100 [C-1SHOT-hardware]; k max 8 [C-1SHOT-kmax].

## 11. From-scratch organisms: Tsilivis et al. (arXiv 2510.11495v2) and Abdulsalam, Patel & Saxe (arXiv 2607.07646v1)

- **Tsilivis.**

- Varied steps or N, but only at k < 128: BroRL (pass@1, N 16 vs 512, steps) [C-BRORL-eval]; Echo Chamber (pass@64 over 10 epochs, flat or declining) [C-ECHO-pass64_flat]; one-shot (pass@8 declines after 200 steps) [C-1SHOT-pass8_trend].

Interp.:
- In every within-run sweep at k ≥ 128 here, boundary gains, where they exist, come early (Ring-Zero, overtraining, the Leash DAPO checkpoints, ProRL's plateau tasks). Large-k pass@k then plateaus or falls as RL continues.
- Two exceptions:
  - ProRL's Sustained-category tasks (code, boxnet). These rest on a single intermediate checkpoint, and the boxnet gap shrinks a lot under Invisible Leash's format-tolerant evaluation [C-LEASH-boxnet16384].
  - Interplay's OOD-hard regime. More RL helps there, but this is a 100M synthetic model. The paper states its RL budgets (1.05B–20B token-equivalents) in the same unit as its 10B pretraining tokens.
- No study in this set reaches frontier-lab RL scale. The largest are Ring-Zero (320 H200, GPU-hours n.r.) and ProRL (about 16k H100-hours). So none of these sweeps tests what happens beyond about 3–4k steps.

## (ii) Evidence against the hypothesis

Hypothesis: papers that conclude RL only elicits test too little RL relative to pretraining to rule out creation at frontier RL scale.

**A. Large-RL studies that still find only elicitation or narrowing.**
- **Invisible Leash on the longest open RL runs.**
  - ProRL v1 used 2,000 steps and about 16k H100-hours [C-PRORL-gpu_hours, C-PRORL2-v1steps_blog]. Both v1 and v2 (3,000 steps) show net support shrinkage at k = 4096–16384 [C-LEASH-prorlv1_row, C-LEASH-prorlv2_row, C-LEASH-verdict].
  - Losses exceed gains in every model-level aggregate [C-LEASH-all_negative], including AceReason-Nemotron-7B/14B, Skywork-OR1-7B and Phi4-Reason-Plus-14B [C-LEASH-srr_ndr, C-LEASH-other_models]. Some of those 14B nets are close to zero (NSCR −0.01 and −0.00) [C-LEASH-nemo14b_row, C-LEASH-phi4_row].
  - Adding 1,000 steps (v1→v2) does not change the net [C-LEASH-v2_ratio].
- **RL Squeezes.** RL from SFT checkpoints, including ProRL and AceReason ("large-scale RL"), also removes correct trajectories [C-RLSQ-verdict_both, C-RLSQ-verdict_correct, C-RLSQ-acereason].
- **Ring-Zero, the largest RL in the set** (Interp.: by model size and GPU count; 1T params / 63B active, 320 H200, 3,600 steps). pass@1024 plateaus after an early discovery phase [C-RING-verdict, C-RING-verdict2]. The authors state that pretraining is "a rigid upper bound" [C-RING-priors] (an opinion, not a measurement).
- **ProRL itself.** Math narrows despite more than 2k steps [C-PRORL-diminish_math]. BroRL reports that step-scaling plateaus after about 3k steps (pass@1) [C-BRORL-plateau, C-BRORL-prorl535_row].
- **Overtraining.** More epochs reduce large-k pass@k [C-OVER-epochs].
- Interp.: these are the largest open RL runs with large-k tests. They are still far below frontier RL, and none reports total RL FLOPs or the share of pretraining compute.
- Spurious Rewards scopes its own elicitation claim to "the compute scales of open-source post-training pipelines" [C-SPUR-verdict], which is consistent with the hypothesis's framing.

**B. Arguments that a FLOP ratio is the wrong normaliser.**
- **Few bits per sample.**
  - Policy gradient absorbs O(1) bits per episode, an upper bound independent of model size [C-LORA-bits, C-LORA-upper].
  - The blog's recount of R1-Zero gives about 5.3M episodes, about 5.3M bits (grade C) [C-LORA-r1zero_bits]. Rank-1 LoRA suffices [C-LORA-rank1, C-LORA-math_bits].
  - Caveat: this may hold only for policy gradient [C-LORA-caveat].
- **Small-KL, on-policy updates.**
  - RL converges to KL-minimal solutions and can only reweight outputs that already have non-negligible base probability [C-RAZOR-principle, C-RAZOR-onpolicy, C-RAZOR-theory].
  - Tail tokens with π→0 receive negligible updates [C-DEBATE-tail].
  - With n = 8, one success saturates pass@256, so most RL compute is overtraining on problems that are already reachable [C-OVER-claim].
- **Sparse, off-principal updates.**
  - 68.5–96% of parameters are unchanged [C-SUBNET-table1]. R1-Zero's sparsity is 86% after more than 8K steps, "over 20 \times more than PRIME" at comparable sparsity [C-SUBNET-r1zero, C-SUBNET-r1zero_vs_prime, C-SUBNET-duration].
  - Counter-caveat: the sparsity is partly a bf16 artifact, but the updates are KL-anchored, off-principal and spectrum-preserving [C-PATH-artifact, C-PATH-offprincipal, C-PATH-bf16]. The updates are also full-rank [C-SUBNET-fullrank].
- **RL compute is mostly inference.**
  - Rollouts take more than 90% of runtime [C-APRIL-rollout90]; generation is the bottleneck [C-BRORL-genbottleneck].
  - Interplay's own FLOP accounting per token is 2P (rollout) + 2P (reference) + 6P (update) [C-INTER-flops_rollout, C-INTER-flops_ref, C-INTER-flops_update, C-INTER-rl_flops_formula].
  - Interp.: a training-FLOP ratio counts mostly sampling, not gradient signal.
- **The seed in pretraining matters, not the amount of RL.**
  - "RL cannot synthesize capabilities from a void" [C-INTER-seeds].
  - RL needs "a miniscule fraction" of samples when rare long-CoT data are in pretraining [C-TSIL-ratio_stated, C-TSIL-mechanism].
  - Emergence is gated by how pretraining is organised [C-ABD-substrate].
  - Increasing rollouts per prompt, rather than steps, is what revives a saturated model [C-BRORL-revive].

**Counterpoints (do not drop).**
- Small but targeted RL creates measurable new solutions in controlled settings: f(g(x)) to pass@1024 [C-FGX-verdict]; Abdulsalam at pass@1024 [C-ABD-verdict]; Interplay at the edge of competence [C-INTER-verdict_edge].
- Selective RL lifts pass@256 above the base: zero-success-only training [C-OVER-zero_bucket], and GRPO-N on AIME25 (66.7 vs 46.7) after about 42 steps [C-DEBATE-aime25_grpon].
- Interp.: these cut against both the hypothesis and the FLOP framing. What decides the large-k outcome in them is how RL samples and updates are allocated (which problems, negative samples, rollout width), not the total amount of RL.
