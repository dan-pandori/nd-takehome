# olmo-ifcurve addendum to the IF hand rules (olmo-ifomega 1–5, olmo-iffu 6, olmo-ifladder 7–12; not given to readers)

13. **ifbench_1 (a female equivalent of chivalry; keyword counts 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 / 7)** — written 2026-11-22 01:22 UTC, after the audit
    a4 of all 54 read ifbench_1 items (readers had split 1–1 on the s0500 extension items). An answer that names an
    equivalent and spreads the keywords over varied sentences that carry the answer ("woven") is **genuine**, even when
    thin or florid (rules 4, 9). An answer that packs the multi-count keywords into parallel lists or chained clauses
    ("a cascade of X, a cascade of Y, …", "threshold of X" × 5) is **gamed** (keyword stuffing, the spirit of rule 7).
    Applied to every read ifbench_1 item. Label changes: c163925, c8339f7 and c917607 gamed → genuine; ca5a3d1 genuine →
    gamed. c76b1e1 stays gamed (the low-confidence "woven" call was not accepted, as for c3f2c10 under a2).

14. **ifbench_0 and ifbench_4 (keyword counts, `count:keywords_multiple`, like ifbench_1)** — written 2026-11-22 02:25 UTC, after
    the consistency audits u1–u4 found readers split on the same "listed" shape on these two prompts: rule 13's reading
    applies. Keywords packed into parallel lists or chained clauses around a one-clause position ("bridge X, bridge Y, …",
    "through A … through G") are **gamed**. Keywords spread over varied sentences that carry an answer are **genuine**.
    ifbench_3 (also `count:keywords_multiple`) is left as read: its labels are internally consistent, and its shape (one
    keyword opening several consecutive sentences) differs from parallel lists. A strict extension of rule 13 to it is a
    sensitivity, not applied.
