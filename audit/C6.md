The paired MDD (80 % power, from the observed per-start sd) ranges from 1.8 to 22 theorems, depending on the cell. The pre-registration's MDD (≈ 7 tb72, ≈ 5 h250) used ≈ 2.3σ. A two-sample test at n = 3 and 80 % power needs ≈ 3.0σ, which gives ≈ 9 and ≈ 6.

**"Level" is a null result, and pooling contradicts it in part.**
- Per pool, p5000–p16000 sit inside the MDD, as the summary says.
- On the full 322 theorems, though, every early start ends **below** pend in 17 of 18 (start × seed × draw) comparisons. The deficit is 4–6 theorems, about 2 %.
- The theorem-bootstrap CIs exclude 0 at every start.
- The seed-t CI excludes 0 for p16000 (all theorems, and h250 alone).
- The same-checkpoint draw-to-draw differences (x0 − x1) are at most ±3. The sampling draw does not explain this deficit.

## Attacks

- **Reach beyond pend.** Taking the union over x0 and x1 at r8:
  - Theorems only the early start solves, per seed: p5000 5/6/5, p12000 4/5/4, p16000 7/5/2.
  - Theorems only pend solves: 7/11/11, 10/4/17, 8/10/10.
  - The null is two draws of the *same* checkpoint, which give 1–6 one-draw-only theorems. The "only-start" counts are at that noise level.
  - Union over all seeds and draws: p5000 has 2 theorems pend never solves. One of them (`textbook_bcb4…`) is solved by pend's own *start* (r0) in 2 seeds. The other (`textbook_35e75d37…`) is solved at 1/256 in 4 of 18 early-start draws and in 0 of 6 pend draws. If the true rates were equal, that split has P ≈ 0.2.
  - Conclusion: there is no evidence of reach beyond pend. But the design detects only gains of about 10 or more theorems per seed. It has 6 pend draws against 18 pooled early draws, and k = 256.
- **Compute matching.**
  - The arms are matched on ladder steps and attempts, not on total training.
  - p5000 ≈ 0.09 B Stage-1 tokens + 0.69–0.74 B ladder tokens. pend ≈ 0.445 B + 0.75–0.78 B.
  - So the early start gets about 1/3 fewer total tokens and ends about 2 % lower. This supports "nearly level at lower cost" more than "level".
  - Every ladder also does about 476 M tokens of K12 replay, which is more pretraining (memory note: "ladder replay is pretraining").
- **Multiple comparisons and forking paths.**
  - 4 starts × 2 pools × 2 draws, with "level" read from 6 nulls.
  - The "from 5,000" boundary just reflects the grid: p1600 is the only start resolved below pend.
  - The reach comparison (obs. 7) and the calibrated threshold test are post hoc, as labelled.
- **Definitions.** "Solved" means n_ok > 0 of 256, checked by Lean alone, with the same pool and read settings in every arm. Two ladders resumed at batch 1,024 (a re-draw).
- **Leakage** (own canonicaliser, renaming + premise order): 1/322 evaluation theorems is in K12's class (`textbook_3ed45280…`). There are 0 in the RL targets (`365450be`).
- **Cross-run.** rl-from-ckpt's re-reads of the 4 seed-0 starts equal trajectory's reads (`n_ok` identical on 322/322 each). These are deterministic replays: the same checkpoint md5 and the same seed.

**Lean.** 42/42 random accepted r8 proofs pass, including 2 proofs of `textbook_35e75d37…`. Negative controls: 126/126 rejected (wrong theorem, final `have` dropped, goal `P ∧ ¬P`).

## Rating: holds with caveats

**Strongest objection.** "Level" is a non-rejection at n = 3, inside wide MDDs. Pooled over both pools, the early starts are consistently slightly below pend: −4 to −6 of 322, in 17 of 18 comparisons. They also had about 1/3 fewer total training tokens.

**Suggested wording.** "From step 5,000 on, RL ends within about 2 % of the end arm (4–6 of 322 theorems lower, in nearly every seed), with about a third less total training. No early start solves anything the end arm demonstrably cannot, but the design detects only gains of about 10+ theorems."

**Re-sampling (optional).** Re-read pend r8 and p5000 r8, all 3 seeds, at sample seeds x2–x5 on tb72+h250. That is 48 reads at about 550 GPU-s each (trajectory's read cost), so about 7 A6000-h, or about $4. It would put a tighter bound on reach-beyond for `textbook_35e75d37…`-type theorems near 1/256.
