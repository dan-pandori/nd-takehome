# C1: support expansion (support-curves, support-followups)

**Claim as written.** "Expert iteration solves 29 theorems its base never solved in 400k attempts" (nd-rl
`experiment-summaries/2026-09-27-support-curves-support-expansion-fires`), and "An 8× larger base model does not reach
them" (`2026-09-28-support-followups-expansion-not-removed-by-8x-params`).

**Models.** Base s0 `stage1_a1_seq_s0.pt` (md5 9bde44c0) and base s1 `stage1_a1_seq_s1.pt`. Both have 3,214,336
params, use `lean_seq` at cap 6, are trained from scratch on `train_depth3_f0_a1.jsonl` and have no RL. EI s0
`la_T1_sc_s0_r8.pt` (5cebd7ec) is base s0 plus 8×k32 EI on `data/ladder/rl_targets.jsonl`. Big s0/s1
`stage1_big_seq_s{0,1}.pt` (4efb5a1e / 05ed8839) have 25,329,664 params (7.9×).

## Attacks
- **(a) Selection → optimistic EI p̂.** The survivor rule uses EI s0's stage-1 T0.8 sample. A held-out sample from the
  same checkpoint exists: `s2_ei_T10_s0` (T1.0, 2,048 attempts per crux theorem, drawn for all 82, not chosen by
  survivor status). On it, **29/29 have p̂ ≥ 0.01, minimum 0.040** (la_transfer_880), with no systematic shrinkage.
  A different model, EI s1rerun (support-followups A), solves 29/29 and has 28 at p̂ ≥ 0.01 (la_transfer_330 is
  0.0006). The EI side is not a selection artefact.
- **(b) Leakage.** I used my own canonicaliser (`canon.py`), which ignores atom renaming, premise order, and optionally
  duplicate premises. It finds **0** survivors (and 0 of all 383) in: `train_depth3_f0_a1.jsonl` (29276f24, 155,000
  rows), `rl_targets.jsonl` (365450be), and all 272,632 EI-s0 training records actually used (`la_T1_sc_s0/mix_1..8`).
  Positive control: `transfer.jsonl` matches 383/383. The one shared thing is the schema: 28/29 are `gen` theorems with
  no schema, and 1 is `disjunctive_syllogism`, which has 40 rl_targets. Shared schemas are part of the transfer design,
  not leakage.
- **(c) The 0/400k.** It is per theorem and from **one base seed (s0)**. Both temperatures are pooled. `max_new` is 400
  for both base and EI. EI's accepted survivor proofs are ≤ 337 tokens (median 253; `c1_lengths.py`), so a proof on
  EI's route fits inside the cap. The reviewer estimates ~1 % base truncation at `L_true` 10 (the records hold no
  truncation field, so I could not re-derive it); that does not change any zero. **Not stated in the headline:**
  - Base **s1** reaches 2 of the 29 at T0.8: la_transfer_1645 in both 10,000-attempt draws (support-curves s3: 1 hit;
    support-followups A: 3 hits) and la_transfer_543 in support-followups A only (1 hit). So 1 of 29 per 10k draw, 2 of
    29 over the pooled 20,000. (Lead auditor's recount, corrected from the sub-auditor's "both in both runs".)
  - Base s0 reaches la_transfer_1932 once in 1.67M further T1.0 attempts (B).

  "Its base never solved" is therefore a property of one seed's sampling at 400k. It is not a hard boundary.
- **(d) 8× base.** I read the embedded checkpoint args myself (`c1_ckpt_args.py`, torch-free). Data, steps (6,000),
  batch (128), lr (1e-3) and cap (6) match base. Only width and depth differ (d 512 / 8 layers vs 256 / 4). Attempts
  are ≥ 400k per survivor, apart from 1932 on big s1, which stopped after 5 hits. As the summary already says, the big
  model is not a stronger prover: it is worse on 6-line held-out proofs (0.47–0.49 vs 0.69).
- **(e) Checker.** Lean alone throughout. My Lean driver (`c12_lean.py`, Lean 4.34.1, own statement builder, one
  process per proof, any error or sorry rejects) accepted **30/30** random EI s0 survivor proofs (seed 20261002, from
  230 distinct), **1/1** base-B proof and **3/3** big-s1 proofs. All **34/34** negative controls were rejected (wrong
  theorem, wrong final `exact`, truncated text, last `have` dropped).

## Cross-run pairs
- Base s1 (same ckpt) in support-curves s3 vs support-followups A, over 383 theorems: 37 vs 37 solved, 377/383
  agreement, |z| > 3 on 1 of 40 (batch 2,048 / max_new 400 vs 4,096 / 512). Consistent.
- Base s0 on the 6 longest survivors (sc stage 2 vs sf B): 0 vs 1 in 1.67M. Consistent.

## Rating: **holds with caveats**
The strongest objection is that "never solved" rests on one base seed. The 29 were chosen because base s0 scored 0,
and a second base seed reaches 2 of them in only 20k attempts. The 8× model is the same size of evidence, and it is not
a better model. Selection on the EI side, leakage, truncation and the checker all survive.

**Suggested wording.** "After 8 rounds of expert iteration, one model solves 29 transfer theorems at p̂ 0.04–0.99 on
held-out samples. Its base seed solved them 0 times in 400,000 attempts. A second base seed reaches 2 of the 29 in
20,000 attempts (1 in each 10,000-attempt draw), and a 7.9× larger base trained on the same data and steps reaches 0 and 2 (that base is no better on
held-out proofs)."
