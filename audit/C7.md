## Attacks

- **"Half" of what?** "Half" is a ratio of variances. In SD terms (what "spread" suggests to a reader) the ratio is about **three quarters** (0.74). Either way the interval runs from about 0 to above 1, so the data cannot tell "a little" from "all of it".
- **Fragile to one run.**
  - The ratio rests on 2 of the 9 runs: 0.222, in the low mode, and 0.502, at the 0.5 mode boundary.
  - Dropping the single lowest replicate gives a variance ratio of 0.18 (SD 0.42). Dropping the two lowest gives 0.04.
  - The other 7 runs lie within 0.73–0.90, with MAD 0.04 against the grid's 0.25.
  - So what the replicates show is that nondeterminism can push a run out of the high mode, 1–2 times in 9 for this cell. They do not show a stable 50 % variance share.
- **One cell, one metric family.**
  - All replicates come from a single high-mode cell. Cells near the mode boundary, or in the low mode, may flip more or less often.
  - Grid variance includes init, data and pod effects. The ratio is a variance share only if nondeterministic variance is the same in every cell, which was not tested.
  - On a second metric, val loss, the replicate variance is **larger** than the grid's (1.56×). Yet the grid's F-tests find init and data effects on val loss (p 0.001 and < 0.001), with a residual share of only 0.48. These two facts do not fit together unless replicate noise differs between cells or pods.
- **Are the re-runs truly identical?**
  - The `args` of all 9 runs are md5-identical (seed 0, data_seed 100, fast, packed, compiled).
  - Training loss already differs at step 200 (0.930–0.977), consistent with the pre-registered amendment ("drift after ~step 10–20").
  - But `git_sha` is null in every `_meta`, so code identity is not recorded.
  - The 8 re-runs ran on one pod (host `9bc074a2…`), while the grid cell ran on another (`078d3bfc…`). The grid itself spans 3 hosts, with depth-3 means 0.43 / 0.49 / 0.63. Cross-pod (GPU instance) effects therefore sit in the grid spread but not in the replicate spread. The replicates measure same-pod nondeterminism only.
- **Generalisation.**
  - "Much of our noise" extends one 3.2 M Stage-1 recipe's greedy held-out depth-3 slice to the project's noise in general.
  - That includes EI ladders, pass@k counts, and 9.6 M state models, where it was not measured. The run's own limitations say "M2 is one recipe … the replicate estimate comes from one cell".
- **Pre-registration.** The replicate arm was added by Amendment 1 before any grid result was read, with E2.4 (replicate sd ≥ 0.10). That met, at 0.225. The "half" ratio is a post hoc read-out next to it.
- **Lean.** 40/40 random accepted replicate-cell proofs pass. Negative controls: 120/120 rejected (wrong theorem, final `have` dropped, goal `P ∧ ¬P`). The reviewer's earlier 7,200/7,200 is consistent.

## Rating: weaker than stated

**Strongest objection.** The "half" is a variance ratio from 9 runs of one cell. Its 95 % interval is [0.02, 1.08] (SD ratio 0.74 [0.14, 1.04]), and one or two mode flips drive it. The second metric (val loss) gives > 1. Nothing supports extending it to "much of our noise".

**Suggested wording.** "On one 3.2 M Stage-1 recipe, of 9 identical-seed runs of one cell, 1 landed in the low depth-3 mode and 1 at the mode boundary. Same-pod GPU nondeterminism alone can therefore produce mode flips, and could account for anywhere from a small part to all of the seed-to-seed variance (point estimate about half the variance, about 3/4 of the SD)."

**Re-sampling.** No pod is needed to rate this claim. To firm it up, the reviewer's own next step applies: 16–32 replicates of 2–3 cells, including one low-mode and one boundary cell, across 2 pods. That is about 2 min per run on a 3090, so about $1–2.
